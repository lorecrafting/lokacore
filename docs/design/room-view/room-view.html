The room view

Round six. The tiny map is the joystick: press it to zoom, drag to walk, and drag out to the stair nodes for up and down. The “You can go” line is gone. Every room is a fresh page: moving or looking turns the page and clears it, and events pile up under the room until you leave. Tap the room title to look. People and things are listed under the description. The prompt sits in the bottom bar with a small centered compass. Anything you open turns to a new page of the book instead of sliding up a menu. The minimap in the compass center opens the Contents.

Anatomy, top to bottom

This is also the screen-reader order.

  1. Room title, which is also Look

    Set in Fell and pinned while events scroll under it. Tapping it turns to a fresh page of the same room, just like typing look. In the dark it reads “Darkness”.

  2. Description

    Set in Garamond. Only the details are dotted (the post, the elm, the reeds); tapping one adds what you see to the log without leaving the page. Exits are left to the compass.

  3. Here lines

    MUD-style, directly under the description: “Bram the ferryman stands here, one boot on the ferry.” Each line is one tap target and turns to that person’s or thing’s page. In the dark the lines become verbs: Feel around in the dark, Light your lantern.

  4. Event log

    Results, dialogue and rejections with their reason tags pile up under the description. Leaving the room, or looking, turns to a clean page, and the full history lives in the text drawer.

  5. Map between long rules

    A tiny endpaper map in place of a fleuron. You are the solid dot and places are open rings, reaching two steps out, with the paths meeting each ring at its edge.

  6. The map is the joystick

    Press the tiny map and it zooms up. Drag toward a path to light it, then let go to walk. Drag back to the middle to cancel. For up and down, drag out to the stair nodes that appear on the right. A plain tap opens the Map page. A tip bubble shows until you first walk, and screen readers get hidden “Go north” buttons.

  7. Status

    One line, from the MUD prompt: the hour, your position (tap to change it) and hp/ma/mv as current/max. Tapping the numbers turns to Character. A small handle below opens the text drawer, and so does swiping up on the footer.

Pages, not menus
Every tap that used to open a sheet now turns to a new page: a person or thing, a conversation, the Contents and each section of it. The turn is a single transform lasting 0.4 seconds, cheap on a Pixel 3a, and it is switched off under reduced motion or the Settings toggle. The text drawer is the one exception. It stays a drawer because it holds the keyboard.
Contents
The book’s contents page, with dotted leaders, covers the map, carrying, equipment, character, journal and settings. Equipment is a plain LegendMUD-style list of 14 slots. Tapping a filled slot turns to that item’s page.
Discoverability risk
Tapping the compass center to reach the Contents can’t be seen from the screen. For the P5 test (a non-developer, no instructions), it needs a hint the first time, or a visible book mark on the minimap ring.

GameView needs, new this round

These add to items 1–15 from earlier rounds.

  1. A presence line per entity. A short “is here” sentence for each person and thing (“Bram the ferryman stands here, one boot on the ferry”), which can change with state. It is separate from the long description and from the room prose, which no longer mentions entities.
  2. Details kept apart from entities. Inspectable details stay anchored in the prose (item 3), while people and things come as a list. The view needs to mark which is which.
  3. Equipment projection. An ordered slot list with a label (“used as light”, “worn on feet”) and the item or empty for each slot, plus compare hints later. A LegendMUD-style list needs nothing more.
  4. Carrying summary. The items, current weight and capacity, and coins. The Contents page counts come from this and the equipment list.
  5. Character sheet. Level, ancestry, resources with bands (see item 14), attributes and faction standing, all as display values. The UI does no arithmetic on them.
