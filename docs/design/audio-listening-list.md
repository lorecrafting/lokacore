# Audio listening list (2026-10-31)

The owner's guide to choosing the game's placeholder music and sounds by ear. Research and writing only: nothing is
downloaded into the app, no Beads filed, no code touched. Rules that bind every row: all audio is
[human-made](../decisions/owner-decision-human-made-content-2026-10-31.md); CC0 or CC BY only; CC BY credits go
in the manifest and on the credits page ([plan, section 14](effects-and-sound-plan.md#14-music-battle-music-and-cutscenes-proposed-2026-10-31-details-open)).
The earlier shortlist, [placeholder audio candidates](placeholder-audio-candidates.md), keeps only the non-Buckley
music and sound-pack rows; everything about Buckley, the stinger, the fanfare and paper sounds now lives here.

**Heard vs read.** Nothing in this document was listened to. Every mood, instrument and timestamp is the composer's
or uploader's own words from the linked page, and "needs listening" marks where only the ear can decide. The one
file analysed was a single Buckley MP3, opened only to read its bitrate so that lengths could be estimated; lengths
in the appendix are computed from each file's size and header bitrate, so treat them as about right to within a
few seconds.

## 1. Decisions

### Music: one composer, one palette

1. **Scott Buckley is the score.** His library has 267 tracks (appendix A; how the count was established is
   there). 23 are first picks, 152 are alternates, 92 are not used, each with its reason. Enough of the catalogue
   fits that no second composer is needed for v1; the non-Buckley rows in the old shortlist stay as fallbacks only.
2. **The palette is his chamber voice.** To sound like one world (and like the owner's Morrowind reference), the
   score uses his strings, piano, solo cello and violin, clarinet, recorder, duduk, harp, choir and soft pads, with
   orchestral percussion only in fights. It excludes his synth leads, electronic drums, electric guitar, drum-kit
   pop, lyrics, comedy, Christmas and sci-fi pieces. A track that carries one of those is marked in brackets in the
   tables and is an alternate at most.
3. **Fights need cuts; cuts need a credit note.** Almost every track is 2-6 minutes and builds; the plan wants a
   60-90 s loop for ordinary fights, a 1-2 s stinger and a 2-4 s fanfare. None of his tracks is published as a
   loop. CC BY 4.0 allows adapting, but the credit must say the work was changed, so the manifest `credit` for any
   cut reads `'Title' by Scott Buckley - released under CC-BY 4.0. www.scottbuckley.com.au (edited: cut and looped)`.
   Whether a clean loop point exists in a given track is a listening question; section 3 says where to look.
4. **Licence and credit, from his pages (read 2026-10-31).** Every one of the 267 track pages carries the CC BY 4.0
   badge and this attribution format: `'Track Title' by Scott Buckley - released under CC-BY 4.0. www.scottbuckley.com.au`
   ([Using this music](https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/using-this-music/) also accepts `Music by Scott Buckley`
   or `Additional Music by Scott Buckley`). Free for commercial use with the credit. Restrictions: the music may not be
   resold on its own or uploaded to streaming or distribution services, and must be synchronised with other media
   (a game is). Remixes are allowed with attribution but must never be submitted to Content ID or similar
   fingerprinting. Nothing in the 267 is excluded or differently licensed; his
   [orchestral remixes](https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/remixes) of other composers' themes live on a separate page
   and are not CC, and none of them is in the library. One track re-orchestrates a traditional carol
   (O Come All Ye Faithful); it is not used.
5. **Useful facts for later.** [Paid licences](https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/licensing/) exist only for
   projects that cannot credit (AUD 40, 75 or 300 per track); with a credit "the CC-BY license still stands, and it's
   free". WAV masters are on a Patreon-locked index page (USD 10 tier). No stems or loops are offered anywhere on the
   site. He takes commissions (game scores for Overhype and Stutter Fox studios, Alan Becker's series; one library
   track, Sanctuary, was "commissioned by an anonymous fan").
6. **Human-made evidence.** No public statement by him about AI was found (two web searches, his FAQ, About and
   licensing pages). The evidence is circumstantial but strong: composing professionally since 2003; commissioned
   scores for named studios; 185 of the 267 tracks were published before 2022; track notes name live elements
   (Hardanger fiddle, his own vocals, cicadas recorded on a family farm, beach field recordings) and a 2021
   Orchestral Tools competition placing. He writes with sample libraries ("my modern orchestral template"), which
   is human composition. Record this in each manifest row's `human_made_evidence`.

### Sound: one family for the log

The owner asked whether log-line events can share one sound. Decision: one **family**, not one sound, built from
paper, coin and the score's own note. Owner changes, 2026-10-31: coins clink, and skill improving and quest
gained have sounds. The rule that keeps a long read quiet: **a sound marks what the hand did or how the player's
story moved; never what the world did.** One tone means something started or moved on; two tones mean it finished or the player grew.
Everything the world does (an NPC leaves, a fact is learned, a hint changes, a room appears on the map) stays
silent, because in a text game read for an hour, a sound on every log line becomes the loudest thing in the room
and stops meaning anything. Coalescing (two pickups in 300 ms are one flick) and the "(N times)" merge already
cap the rate.

| Event | Sound | Severity | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pickup, any item into the hand | **flick** (one recorded paper flick, 3 variants) | S2 | the hand did it; the owner's chosen moment |
| Buy, sell, any pennies in or out | **coin** (one short clink, one sample) | S2 | money should sound like money; a purchase is one coin, no flick |
| Status applied (debuff only) | **tick** (same sample) | S2 | the icon's arrival needs a nudge |
| Level up | **note, high** (two rising tones: the main theme's first interval) | S3 | the one "you grew" moment |
| New skill learned | **note, low** (the same two tones a fifth down) | S2 | kin to a quest closing, smaller than a level |
| Quest resolved | **note, low** (shared with new skill) | S3 | same |
| Quest failed, deadline passed | **close** (book shut softly) | S2 | an ending without reward |
| Skill improves | **tone, high** (one tone, quiet), at most once per 30 s; *gilt* on the line | S2 | growth is heard; the limit stops grinding nagging |
| Quest gained | **tone, low** (one tone); the running head cross-fades | S2 | a question that quest resolved's two tones answer |
| Hint change | silent; the running head cross-fades | S2 | the book turned to it |
| Knowledge, fact, rumour, reputation | silent | S0 | owner rule |
| New map room | silent; the ink draws | S2 | the drawing is the sound |
| Generic narrated event | silent; the line dries | S1 | the prose is the effect |

So the whole log vocabulary is flick, coin, tick, one or two tones, high or low, and close. The tones are the only pitched
sounds outside music and fights, and they come from the main theme so that a level feels like the score, not a
phone. First try to cut them from the chosen theme (needs listening); failing that, a recorded harp or plucked
string from section 4.

### Stinger and fanfare

Try Buckley's own material first, so the fight sounds like the same composer as the room. Text evidence names
these moments (none heard): a stinger from the opening of The Fall ("a dark brass chord signals a dark change"),
from Fading ("one huge string rise"), or from Time and Space's drop "at ~2:05"; a fanfare from the opening of
Hyperion ("starts with a brass fanfare"), the finale of Clarion ("a fanfare-like brass and choir finale") or the
finish of Victor Lux ("a big symphonic finish"). If none cuts cleanly, the CC0 fallbacks in section 4 apply; all
are weaker than a Buckley cut because they are another voice.

category "Music" and every one with the audio format. The human listing shows "Page 1 of 14": thirteen pages of
twenty plus seven on page 14 is 267, and the seven slugs on the last page are all in the set. The category term
