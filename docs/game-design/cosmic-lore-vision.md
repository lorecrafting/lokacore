# Part 1: The Essential Foundation

## 1.1 The Present-Day Situation

**Question:** What is the state of the universe when players arrive?

**Decision:** ✅ DECIDED - "The Last Ships Out" / Frontier Exodus

### The Core Worlds (Old Worlds)
The established civilizations are **falling apart**:
- Open wars between major factions
- Environmental collapse on some worlds
- Economic systems breaking down
- Authoritarian crackdowns as order fails
- The gate network becoming unstable in core regions

### The Frontier Worlds (Where Players Are)
Recently rediscovered planets represent **salvation**:
- Gates to "lost" planets have been reopened
- One-way journeys (gates unstable, return not guaranteed)
- Limited capacity - not everyone could go
- Those who left knew they probably weren't coming back

### Tone: "Clear but Distant"
- The collapse is **real and referenced** - it happened, it's why you're here
- But it's "back there" - news arrives sometimes, refugees with stories
- The focus is on **building the future**, not dwelling on the past
- Occasional reminders of why you left (keeps stakes present)

### The Emotional Core
> *"The old world is burning. You could have stayed and fought over the ashes. Instead, you chose to build something new. The question is: can we do better this time?"*

### Future Content Hook
- Return to old worlds is **theoretically possible** (gates work both ways)
- Not available at launch
- Leaves door open for "return to the core worlds" expansion
- Could be dramatic: rescue missions, scavenging, seeing what's left

**Options considered:**
- [x] Frontier Era with darker undertone ← CHOSEN
- [ ] Golden Age - Peace and prosperity, focus on building
- [ ] Recovery - Rebuilding after some past crisis
- [ ] Tension - Peaceful but political undercurrents

## 1.2 Player Awakening

**Question:** How do players enter this world?

**Decision:** ✅ DECIDED - Unique arrival per planet, core motivation memory only

### The Transit
Players fled the collapsing core worlds through different methods, depending on which frontier planet they chose:

| Planet | Arrival Method | Experience |
|--------|----------------|------------|
| **Planet 1** | Biological Seedship | Wake inside what seems like a forest. Slowly realize the "forest" IS the ship - it landed and seeded the planet. Organic, mysterious. |
| **Planet 2** | Consciousness Transfer | "Wake up" from what felt like a long dream. Your body was grown here; your mind was transmitted. Ethereal, tech-spiritual. |
| **Planet 3** | Cryo-Pod Crash | Traditional sci-fi: escape pod crash-landed. Wake in wreckage, must find your way. Gritty, survival-focused. |

### Memory
Players remember their **core motivation** - why they left, the collapse, wanting something better - but personal details are foggy. This provides:
- Clean slate for roleplay
- Shared understanding ("we all know why we're here")
- No need for extensive backstory creation
- The past matters less than what you build now

### Spark Bonding
**Same origin, different appearance:**
- All Sparks are fragments of the ancient gate network
- But they MANIFEST differently based on arrival:
  - **Planet 1:** Emerges from a glowing flower/seed pod
  - **Planet 2:** Coalesces from the fading dream, becomes solid
  - **Planet 3:** Flickers to life from wreckage, salvaged tech aesthetic

This creates consistent lore with varied player experiences.

### Why This Works
- Explains fresh start (transit wiped possessions, fogged memories)
- Justifies choosing any planet (different ships/methods went to different places)
- Each planet has unique "first 5 minutes" experience
- Spark bonding feels natural to each context

## 1.3 Gate Travel

**Question:** How do the gates work and who controls them?

**Decision:** ✅ DECIDED - Hybrid ancient/player system with development-based access

### Travel Frequency: "Earned, Not Given"
- **Early game:** Travel is rare and meaningful. You're committed to your planet.
- **Mid game:** As planets develop, gates stabilize, travel becomes more accessible
- **Late game:** Thriving planets have reliable inter-planetary connection

**Design principle:** Encourage players to develop their home planet before becoming travelers.

### Gate Mechanics

**Cooldowns tied to planetary development:**
- Undeveloped planets: Long personal cooldowns (days?)
- Developing planets: Moderate cooldowns (hours?)
- Thriving planets: Short cooldowns (accessible travel)

**Costs (layered):**
1. **Resource cost:** Rare materials or energy to activate
2. **Progression-gated:** Need certain reputation/standing to access
3. **Cooldown:** Can't just hop back and forth constantly

### Gate Control: Hybrid System
- **Ancient technology:** Gates are self-maintaining relics of a lost civilization
- **Player effort required:** But they need to be stabilized, powered, expanded by player communities
- **Collaborative goal:** Gate stability is a planetary achievement
- **No central authority:** Nobody "owns" the gates, but developed communities maintain them better

### Lore Implications
- The gates existed before humans arrived (who built them?)
- They were dormant/unstable until recently rediscovered
- Frontier planets have gates but they were barely functional
- Player development = civilization literally rebuilding the network
- Unstable gates explain why you can't easily return to core worlds

### Gameplay Benefits
- Encourages planetary loyalty and development
- Travel is a meaningful reward, not a given
- Creates economy around gate resources
- Collaborative goal: "Let's get our gate to tier 3!"
- Late-game opens up inter-planetary trade and community

## 1.4 Cultural Divergence

**Question:** Why did each planet develop such different cultures?

**Decision:** ✅ DECIDED - Combination of intentional colonization + environmental influence

### The Explanation: "Cultural Arks" Shaped by Worlds

**Layer 1: Intentional Seeding (The Past)**
- Centuries/millennia ago, during some earlier crisis or expansion
- Groups deliberately colonized planets to preserve/develop cultural traditions
- "Cultural ark" ships carried people who valued specific ways of life
- Each group chose planets that seemed compatible with their values

**Layer 2: Environmental Reinforcement (The Evolution)**
- Once settled, each planet's environment shaped the culture further
- A forest world reinforced nature-worship and organic thinking
- A harsh world reinforced discipline, hierarchy, survival values
- Generations of adaptation deepened the cultural identity

**Layer 3: Isolation and Divergence**
- Gates were unstable for long periods (dark ages?)
- Each planet evolved in relative isolation
- When gates reopened, cultures had diverged dramatically
- Technology remained universal (shared knowledge), but values/aesthetics/social structures diverged

### Example Application
**Planet 1 (Forest World):**
- Original colonists: People who valued nature, sustainability, community
- Environment: Lush forests, living ecosystems, organic resources
- Evolution: Developed deep ecology practices, organic technology, nature spirituality

**Why Technology is Universal but Culture is Not:**
- Gate network allows knowledge/tech sharing (everyone has starships)
- But VALUES, AESTHETICS, and SOCIAL STRUCTURES are local
- You can share blueprints through a gate, but not identity
- This mirrors real Earth: same technology, vastly different cultures

### Lore Richness
This creates opportunities for:
- "Origin stories" for each planet (who founded it? why?)
- Cultural clashes when people interact
- Questions of identity (are you more "human" or more "your planet"?)
- Ancient history mysteries (the original colonization, the dark ages)

# Part 6: The Cosmic Framework

> **Status:** ✅ CORE DECISIONS MADE
> **Tone:** Present but not explicit. Players feel it before they understand it.

## The Higher Beings

### The Ultimate Truth (Internal Reference Only)
At the highest level, the higher beings are **unified** - not separate entities but aspects of something greater, like facets of a jewel. There is no conflict at that level because there is no separation.

### How They Appear to Mortals
From below, looking up, they **seem** like different beings:
- Some appear nurturing, some fierce, some distant
- Some seem aligned with nature, others with knowledge, others with justice
- Different cultures give them different names and attributes
- They may even seem to be in opposition

**But the benevolent ones never fight each other.** This is a key tell for players who pay attention - every apparent conflict between "good" higher beings resolves into complementary action. Only beings who have fallen from unity (through attachment, desire, or corruption) actually oppose each other.

### Their Relationship to Mortals
- They **cannot directly intervene** - not because of arbitrary rules, but because of their nature
- Direct intervention would overwhelm mortal free will
- Instead, they **influence through small touches**: a dream, a coincidence, a stranger's kindness
- They feel **compassion** but also **patience** - they operate on timescales mortals can't comprehend
- They are not "other" - they are what mortals can **become**

## The Gate Builders

### Who They Were
The gate builders were a civilization that **transcended**:
- They evolved past war and competition
- Not through suppression, but through understanding
- When beings truly understand interconnection, conflict becomes illogical
- This inner peace enabled their outer achievements

### What They Built
- The gate network spans more than physical space
- The gates touch something deeper - perhaps the fabric that connects all consciousness
- Building them required not just technology but **spiritual evolution**
- A warlike civilization could never have built them - the gates require harmony to create

### What Happened to Them
**They didn't die - they ascended.**
- The gate builders reached the level of the higher beings
- They are still present, just not in mortal form
- Some of their essence may persist in their creations
- The Sparks may be fragments of their compassion, left behind to guide those who follow

### The Lesson
> *"The gate builders show us what we could become. Not through conquest, but through letting go of the need to conquer."*

## The Sparks' True Nature

### The Connection
Sparks have an **intuitive connection** to the higher beings and the gate builders:
- They don't consciously remember, but they *feel* things
- Certain places resonate with them (ancient shrines, gate sites)
- Certain actions feel "right" to them without knowing why
- They're drawn to help mortals, even when it serves no practical purpose

### The Awakening Arc
As players progress through the storyline, Sparks gradually remember:

| Stage | What Sparks Know | Player Experience |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Early** | Just instincts - "this feels important" | Cryptic hints, unexplained feelings |
| **Mid** | Fragments - flashes of memory, recognition | "I've seen this before..." moments |
| **Late** | Understanding - the connection becomes clear | Revelations about their origin |
| **Endgame** | Purpose - why they're here, what they're for | The full picture emerges |

### The Mystery
Even fully awakened, Sparks don't have complete knowledge. They're fragments, echoes. The higher beings are patient - understanding comes when understanding is ready.

## The Cycle

### The Pattern
Collapse and rebuilding are part of a vast cycle:
- Civilizations rise, become attached, fight, fall
- This isn't punishment - it's the nature of things
- Each cycle offers opportunities for growth
- Some beings transcend; most return to try again

### This Cycle
The current collapse follows the pattern:
- The old worlds became attached to power, territory, ideology
- Competition replaced cooperation
- The gates became unstable (reflecting the disharmony)
- Collapse was inevitable - perhaps even necessary

### The Opportunity
The frontier worlds represent a chance:
- Fresh start, without the accumulated weight
- But the same seeds exist in all mortals
- Will this cycle be different?
- That depends on choices made now

### The Higher Beings' Role
They watch with compassion:
- They cannot force transcendence
- They can only create conditions, offer hints
- The Sparks are one such gift
- The frontier worlds are another - a clean slate

## How This Manifests In-Game

### Environmental Hints
- Ancient shrines to unnamed beings (who built them? when?)
- Meditation spots where Sparks feel "pulled"
- Natural formations that seem too perfect to be random
- Ruins that predate the gate builders - who came before?

### NPCs
- Wandering figures who give perfect advice, then vanish
- Old hermits whose "nonsense" turns out to be true
- Children who draw future events
- Merchants who appear exactly when needed

### Mechanical Expression (Subtle)
- Actions have consequences that ripple unexpectedly
- Helping others returns to you in unexpected ways
- Greed and cruelty create subtle debts
- Not "karma points" - just the world remembering

### The Reveal Arc
Players should feel before they understand:
1. **Strange** - "That was weird coincidence"
2. **Pattern** - "This keeps happening..."
3. **Wonder** - "Is something watching?"
4. **Understanding** - "Oh... it's not watching. It's *hoping*."

## Design Principles

### Show, Don't Tell
Never use terms like:
- Bodhisattva, karma, dharma, enlightenment
- Jade Emperor, celestial bureaucracy, mandate of heaven
- Any explicit Buddhist/Daoist terminology

Instead, let players feel the concepts through experience.

### The Andor Approach
Keep it grounded:
- Higher beings don't appear dramatically
- No glowing figures giving quests
- Instead: a merchant who knew you'd need this, a dream that warned you
- The cosmic is present in the mundane

### Earned Revelation
- Early game: No explicit cosmic content
- Mid game: Strange things that don't quite add up
- Late game: Pieces start connecting
- Endgame: The full picture, but still with mystery

### Player Agency
The cycle can be broken - or not. Players' collective choices matter:
- Will the frontier worlds repeat the pattern?
- Or will enough beings transcend to shift things?
- This is the ultimate question, never fully answered

## Quick Reference: What's Decided

### The Setting
- **Core worlds:** Collapsing (wars, environmental collapse, chaos)
- **Frontier worlds:** Recently reopened via gates, where players build anew
- **Tone:** "Clear but distant" - the collapse is real but focus is on building the future
- **Theme:** "The old world is burning. What will you build?"
- **Quality bar:** Andor-like grounded production - every detail earns its place

### Player Origin
- Different arrival per planet:
  - Planet 1: Biological seedship (wake in living forest)
  - Planet 2: Consciousness transfer (wake from long dream)
  - Planet 3: Cryo-pod crash (wake in wreckage)
- Memory: Core motivation only (why you left), personal details foggy
- Spark bonds during awakening (same origin, different appearance per planet)

### Gate System
- Ancient technology + player effort to stabilize
- Travel gated by planetary development (encourages building home first)
- Resource cost + progression requirements
- Cooldowns decrease as planet thrives

### Cultural Divergence
- "Cultural arks" - intentional colonization by groups with specific values
- Environment reinforced cultural traits over generations
- Technology universal, but values/aesthetics/social structures diverged

### Starter World
- **Vibe:** Frontier town - "rough but hopeful, everyone pitching in"
- **Appeal:** Community, mutual aid, building something together
- **Shadow:** Conformity pressure, suspicion of those who leave
- **Moral stance:** Gray - good intentions with real costs

### Cosmic Framework
- **Higher beings:** Unified at ultimate level, appear diverse to mortals. Benevolent ones never fight each other. Cannot directly intervene - influence through nudges.
- **Gate builders:** Transcended war and competition. This inner peace enabled their building. They ascended, didn't die. Sparks may be fragments of their compassion.
- **Sparks:** Intuitive connection to higher beings. Gradually awaken through storyline. Drawn to help mortals without knowing why.
- **The cycle:** Collapse and rebuilding are cyclical. Each cycle offers opportunity for transcendence. Current frontier worlds = fresh chance to break the pattern.
- **Manifestation:** Buddhist/Daoist influences on the fringes, never explicit. Show don't tell. Players feel before they understand.
