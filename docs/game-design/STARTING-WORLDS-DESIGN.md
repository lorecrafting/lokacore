# 1. Overview & Goals

## 1.1 What We're Building

A starting world that:
- Appears to be a forest at first
- Gradually reveals itself to be inside a giant **seedship**
- The ship's mission: reseed a dying planet
- "Space druids" aesthetic without the name

## 1.2 Key Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **The Reveal** | Players discover the truth gradually, not through exposition dump |
| **Always-On MMO** | No "replay" - choices matter but don't lock major content |
| **Draw Them In** | Tone should hook players emotionally from the start |
| **Focus** | Design ONE world deeply before thinking about others |

## 1.3 What We're NOT Doing

- Not using the monastery (that was placeholder)
- Not locking major content behind choices (MMO reality)
- Not front-loading weirdness - let it emerge naturally
- Not calling them druids

# 2. Design Pillars

Refined from our research, adapted for this project:

## 2.1 The Gradual Reveal

> "The world is not what it first appears."

Players start believing they're in a simple forest. Clues accumulate:
- Strange materials that aren't quite wood
- "Sky" that flickers at the edges
- Elders who speak of "the mission" in hushed tones
- Areas that are "forbidden" for unclear reasons

The truth: They're inside a massive seedship, a living vessel designed to restore a dying world.

## 2.2 Direction-Based Navigation

> "Learn the world by living in it."

NPCs give directions using landmarks and sensory cues:
- "Follow the stream until you hear the singing stones"
- "The healer lives where the canopy thins and the light changes"

## 2.3 Meaningful Choices (Without Lockout)

> "Your choices shape your story and reputation, but don't gate major content."

In an always-on MMO:
- Choices affect **how** you experience content, not **if**
- Reputation and relationships change based on decisions
- Some side content may vary based on past choices
- Major storylines remain accessible to all

## 2.4 Thematic Depth

> "What is worth saving? What are we willing to sacrifice to save it?"

Central questions for this world:
- What does it mean to be a "keeper" of something dying?
- How do we honor the past while building the future?
- What do we owe to generations we'll never meet?

## 2.5 Environmental Storytelling

> "The forest remembers. The walls have secrets."

- Room descriptions hint at deeper history
- Items carry fragments of the past
- The environment itself is a character

# 3. World 1: The Seedship Forest

## 3.1 The Core Concept

**Surface Level** (What players first experience):
- A lush, ancient forest
- A community of people who tend and protect it
- Something is wrong - a blight, an imbalance, a crisis

**Deeper Truth** (Revealed over time):
- The "forest" is inside a colossal seedship
- The ship has been traveling for generations
- Their mission: carry life to a dying world
- The ship is approaching its destination - or something has gone wrong

## 3.2 The Big Questions

These questions guide our design:

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| What do they call themselves? | Not druids. Tenders? Keepers? Gardeners? Seedborn? |
| Do inhabitants KNOW they're on a ship? | Some elders may, most have forgotten? Or is it common knowledge? |
| What's the crisis when player arrives? | Ship malfunction? Approaching destination? Internal conflict? |
| What's the dying planet? | Their destination? Or where they came from? |
| How long has the journey been? | Generations? Centuries? So long the truth became myth? |
| What is the player? | New arrival? Awakened sleeper? Born on the ship? |

## 3.3 Aesthetic Notes

**Nature + Technology Blend:**
- Organic shapes that are somehow too perfect
- Bioluminescence that pulses with rhythm (ship's heartbeat?)
- Trees that grow in patterns (programmed?)
- "Soil" that has a different texture in places
- Water that flows in ways that don't quite make sense

**Not Fantasy, Not Sci-Fi:**
- Avoid laser beams and terminals
- Avoid pointed hats and spell books
- Something in between - grown technology, living systems

# 4. Worldbuilding Deep Dive

## 4.1 The Seedship

### The Physical Reality

| Aspect | Answer |
|--------|--------|
| **What it is** | A colossal living vessel, part ship, part organism, part garden |
| **Size** | Several kilometers across - a contained biome, not infinite |
| **Interior** | A forest dome with artificial sky, streams, clearings, and one central settlement |
| **Age** | Traveling for 7+ generations (~200 years). Long enough that the origin is story, not memory. |
| **Who built it** | The Ancestors - a civilization facing extinction. Details are myth now. |
| **Destination** | A planet called "The Barren" or "The Wounded World" - a place that was once alive but died |
| **Current status** | Approaching destination. Systems straining. The forest is showing signs of sickness. |

### What the People Call It

They don't call it a ship. To them, it's simply:
- **"The Grove"** - the world, their home
- **"The Cradle"** - poetic/religious term
- The concept of "ship" has been lost to most

### The Living Systems

The seedship is biotechnology - grown, not built:
- The "sky" is a luminescent membrane that cycles day/night
- The "soil" is a living substrate connected to ship systems
- Trees have root networks that interface with the vessel
- Water flows in circuits, recycled and purified
- The whole forest IS the ship's life support

### Clues That Something Is Wrong

| Symptom | What It Really Means |
|---------|---------------------|
| Trees are developing odd growths | Ship systems straining |
| The "sky" flickers at the edges | Hull/membrane stress |
| Some streams are running backward | Gravity systems fluctuating |
| Animals are restless, migrating | They sense what humans don't |
| Elders are meeting in secret | They know the truth |

## 4.2 The People

### Name & Identity

| Aspect | Answer |
|--------|--------|
| **What they call themselves** | **The Tenders** - those who tend the grove |
| **Their purpose (as they understand it)** | To care for the forest so it continues to thrive |
| **What they don't know** | That the forest IS the ship, that they have a destination |

### Social Structure

| Role | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **The Elder Circle** | 5-7 oldest Tenders who guide the community. Some know the truth. |
| **The Rootspeakers** | Those who "listen" to the forest - healers, seers. The companion is one. |
| **The Shapers** | Those who cultivate, prune, guide growth - gardeners, builders |
| **The Gatherers** | Those who harvest what the forest provides - food, materials |
| **The Watchers** | Those who patrol the edges, guard the forbidden zones |

### Knowledge of the Truth

| Group | What They Know |
|-------|----------------|
| **Most Tenders** | Nothing. The Grove is the world. It has always been. |
| **The Watchers** | Something is beyond the edges. They guard it but don't understand. |
| **Some Elders** | The full truth - passed down in secret. They debate when/if to reveal it. |
| **The Rootspeakers** | They sense something deeper. Dreams of movement, of purpose. |

### Daily Life

| Time | Activities |
|------|------------|
| **Waking** | The "sky" brightens. Communal meal. Assignments given. |
| **Day** | Tending work - pruning, planting, harvesting, building, healing |
| **Midday** | Rest in the shade. Stories, songs, connection. |
| **Evening** | Return to the Heartwood. Shared meal. Elders speak. |
| **Night** | The sky dims to bioluminescent glow. Sleep, dreams. |

### Beliefs & Customs

| Belief | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **The Grove provides** | Everything comes from the forest; everything returns to it |
| **The Pulse** | They feel a rhythm in the ground - the ship's heartbeat. Sacred. |
| **The Rootsong** | Rootspeakers can hear the forest "speak" - actually ship systems |
| **The Edges are forbidden** | Where the forest thins, you do not go. Bad things there. |
| **The Ancestors watch** | Those who came before are part of the Grove now |

## 4.3 The Mission

### The Dying Planet

| Aspect | Answer |
|--------|--------|
| **Name** | The Barren / The Wounded World / [their original home?] |
| **What happened** | Ecological collapse - war, pollution, or natural catastrophe |
| **Current state** | Lifeless rock, but with potential for restoration |
| **What's needed** | Seeds, species, soil bacteria, knowledge - everything the Grove carries |

### What the Seedship Carries

- **Living biome**: The forest itself is the cargo - a complete ecosystem
- **Seed vaults**: Deep in the ship, preserved genetic material
- **The Tenders**: The people who will do the work of restoration
- **The Knowledge**: How to heal a world (encoded in Rootspeaker traditions)
- **The Heart**: The soul-seed that will become the planet's consciousness

### The Plan (As the Ancestors Intended)

1. Travel to the Barren (complete)
2. Land the seedship on the surface
3. The Grove becomes the first forest of the new world
4. The Tenders spread life outward from there
5. Over generations, restore the entire planet

### The Timeline

| When | What |
|------|------|
| **200 years ago** | Launch from dying homeworld |
| **7 generations** | Life in the Grove, truth gradually forgotten |
| **Now** | Approaching destination, systems failing |
| **Soon** | Must land or the Grove dies |
| **The problem** | Landing requires a sacrifice the Elders have been dreading |

## 4.4 The Crisis

### The Central Problem

**The ship cannot land safely.**

After 200 years, the living systems are exhausted. The approach is straining everything. Without intervention, one of two things happens:
1. **Crash landing** - The Grove is destroyed, everyone dies
2. **Abort** - Continue drifting, but the Grove dies slowly anyway

### The Solution (Terrible)

To stabilize the landing, the ship needs a **Heart** - a soul willingly given to merge with the vessel's consciousness. This was always the plan. The Ancestors knew.

The Elders have known for generations that someone would have to make this sacrifice. They've been hoping for another way. There isn't one.

### The Player's Entry Point

The player arrives/awakens as the crisis is beginning:
- The forest is visibly sick
- Animals are behaving strangely
- The Elders are tense, secretive
- The companion (a Rootspeaker) is having disturbing visions

The player is drawn into investigating what's wrong - and slowly uncovers the truth alongside the companion.

### Why the Companion Chooses to Sacrifice

This must feel earned, not forced. Possible reasons:
- They've always sensed they were meant for something larger
- Their Rootspeaker abilities make them uniquely suited
- They've seen what the restored world could be - and believe it's worth it
- They love the community too much to let it die
- They choose hope over survival

The player cannot take their place (tried, doesn't work - the ship chooses willing souls).

# 5. Story Structure

## 5.1 The Player's Journey

| Phase | What Happens | What Player Learns |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Arrival** | Player wakes/arrives in the forest | This is a forest, these people tend it |
| **Integration** | Player helps with daily tasks, meets people | Something is wrong, hints of deeper mystery |
| **Companion Bond** | Meet and build relationship with key companion | This person matters to you |
| **First Clue** | Something doesn't add up | Wait, that's not natural... |
| **Investigation** | Player starts asking questions | Pieces of the truth emerge |
| **Revelation** | The truth becomes undeniable | We're on a ship. We have a mission. |
| **Crisis Point** | The ship cannot land safely | Stakes become life or death |
| **The Sacrifice** | Companion gives their heart essence to stabilize landing | Heartbreak. Loss. |
| **Resolution** | Ship lands. Companion's soul imbues the hometown. | Bittersweet. They live on in everything. |

## 5.2 The Companion Sacrifice (Core Emotional Beat)

**The Setup:**
- Player meets companion early in the story
- Genuine rapport is built through shared experiences
- Companion has their own personality, hopes, fears
- Player comes to care about them

**The Crisis:**
- The ship is approaching destination but something is wrong
- It cannot land safely - will crash, killing everyone
- The ship's living systems need a "heart essence" to stabilize

**The Sacrifice:**
- Companion chooses to give themselves
- Not forced, not random - a choice born of love for the community
- Player cannot stop it (or can try but fail?)
- Companion's last moments are meaningful, not rushed

**The Transformation:**
- Companion's soul merges with the ship/hometown
- As the seedship lands and expands into a living town...
- The town IS the companion now, in a sense
- They live on in the streets, the trees, the very air
- NPCs might say "the wind sounds like [companion] laughing"
- The hometown becomes a character, connected to this loss

**Why This Works:**
- Stakes feel real (someone you love dies)
- The loss isn't meaningless (they become the town)
- The hometown has emotional weight forever
- Players will remember this character
- Bittersweet > tragic (there's hope in the transformation)

## 5.3 The Long Arc: Soul Evolution (Late Game Reveal)

**The Deeper Truth (revealed much later):**

As players develop the planet over time - terraforming, building, expanding - a greater truth emerges:

| Phase | What's Happening | What Players See |
|-------|------------------|------------------|
| **Seedship Landing** | Companion sacrifices, soul imbues hometown | Immediate grief, hometown feels "alive" |
| **Early Development** | Town grows, spreads, players build | Town has a presence, personality |
| **Terraforming** | Planet begins to transform | Strange resonances, the land responds |
| **Mid-Game** | Planet develops ecosystems | Hints that something larger is happening |
| **Late Game Reveal** | The planet is becoming sentient | The companion's soul SEEDED the planet |
| **Endgame** | A new planetary consciousness emerges | Companion has evolved into something cosmic |

**The Cosmology:**
- This is a path of soul evolution
- The ultimate form: becoming a sentient planet
- The companion didn't die - they're becoming something greater
- The seedship wasn't just carrying plants - it was carrying the seed of a new planetary soul
- Every planet in this universe may have started this way

**Emotional Payoff:**
- The grief from early game transforms into awe
- "I thought I lost them. They're becoming a god."
- Players' work building the planet is nurturing their friend's evolution
- The planet isn't just a place - it's a being you helped birth
- The companion's personality might emerge in how the planet behaves

**Story Implications:**
- Other planets may have been seeded by other sacrifices
- This reframes the entire mission of the seedships
- What does it mean to have a relationship with a planet-consciousness?
- The companion can "speak" through the world itself

## 5.2 The Reveal Pacing

How do we pace the "we're on a spaceship" revelation?

| Method | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **Environmental** | Edge of forest has strange walls, "sky" flickers |
| **NPC Slips** | Elder uses a word they shouldn't know |
| **Forbidden Zones** | Places you're not allowed to go - why? |
| **Artifacts** | Objects that don't belong in a forest |
| **Dreams/Visions** | Player sees glimpses of the truth |
| **The Crisis** | Whatever is going wrong exposes the reality |

## 7.2 The Companion (Deep Design)

*The most important character in the first arc.*

### Reference: Alchemy of Souls (Korean Drama)

The Naksu/Jang Uk relationship provides a model for building deep bonds:

| Element | How It Worked | Application to Our Story |
|---------|---------------|--------------------------|
| **Role Inversion** | She's his servant but actually his teacher | Companion appears subordinate but becomes guide |
| **Brutal Mentorship** | Her methods are harsh but she's the best | Training moments that push the player |
| **Mutual Vulnerability** | He supports her when powerless, she supports him | Each sees the other's weakness |
| **Life-Risking Saves** | They put lives on the line for each other | Critical moments where one saves the other |
| **Bickering to Love** | Conflict evolves into deep respect | Early tension that softens over time |
| **Light & Shadow** | Their names mean they complete each other | Complementary strengths |
| **Shared Secrets** | The truth about her identity bonds them | What they know together vs. the world |
| **Sacrifice of Selfish Desires** | Both learn to put others first | Growth through letting go |

Sources: [Alchemy of Souls - AsianWiki](https://asianwiki.com/Alchemy_of_Souls), [Kdrama Review](https://kyraganatrasalevelfilmstudiesblog.wordpress.com/2023/01/08/embracing-the-light-and-the-shadows/)

### Design Goals

- Player must genuinely care about them before the sacrifice
- Cannot feel like a plot device - must feel like a person
- Their sacrifice must feel earned, not forced
- They should have flaws, not be a saint
- The relationship evolves through distinct phases

### The Bonding Arc with Thera (12 Phases)

---

#### Phase 1: First Meeting
**"The Healer Finds You"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Player wakes/arrives in the Grove, disoriented, possibly injured |
| **Scene** | Thera is there. Calm, competent, asking gentle questions while checking vitals. |
| **Key moment** | "You're safe now. I'm Thera. Can you tell me your name?" |
| **Tone** | Warmth, safety, being cared for |

**What player feels**: Relief. This person is kind. I can trust them.

---

#### Phase 2: Initial Friction
**"Too Much Help"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Player is recovering, wants to contribute, explore |
| **Scene** | Thera insists player rest more. Player pushes back. Small clash. |
| **Key moment** | Thera: "I've seen people push too hard. They break." Player: "I'm not broken." |
| **Resolution** | Thera relents, but with conditions. Mutual respect established. |

**What player learns**: Thera isn't a pushover. They have opinions. They care maybe too much.

---

#### Phase 3: Thera Saves Player
**"The Thinning Incident"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Player explores too far, gets into danger (creature, environmental hazard, sickness from edge effects) |
| **Scene** | Thera arrives, handles crisis with startling competence. Gets player out. |
| **Key moment** | Thera is breathing hard, spent real energy. "That was foolish. Don't do it again." But they stayed. |
| **Cost** | Thera is visibly drained afterward. They minimize it. |

**What player feels**: Gratitude. And the first glimpse that Thera gives more than they should.

---

#### Phase 4: Teaching
**"The Rootsong Lesson"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Thera offers to teach player about the Grove - how to listen, how to tend, basic healing |
| **Scene** | Training montage moments. Thera is patient, skilled, occasionally amused by player's attempts. |
| **Key moment** | Player succeeds at something small. Thera's genuine smile: "There. You felt it." |
| **Subtext** | Thera loves teaching. When's the last time they had a real student, not just a patient? |

**What player learns**: Respect for Thera's knowledge. The world has depth.

---

#### Phase 5: Player Helps Thera
**"The Breaking Point"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Player finds Thera alone, overwhelmed. Someone needed them, and someone else needed them, and they couldn't be everywhere. |
| **Scene** | Thera is sitting, still, staring at nothing. The mask slipped. |
| **Key moment** | Player can: listen, fetch them food, handle a small task for them, or just sit with them. |
| **Thera's response** | Surprise. "You didn't have to..." Then, quieter: "Thank you." |

**Shift**: Thera realizes the player SEES them. Not just the healer. Them.

---

#### Phase 6: Shared Discovery
**"The Edge Together"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Something is wrong with the Grove. Thera's visions are getting worse. They need answers. |
| **Scene** | Thera asks the player to come with them. To the Thinning. To look at what the Watchers guard. |
| **Key moment** | They see something they can't explain. The sky... flickers. The trees end. There's... metal? |
| **Pact** | "We don't tell anyone. Not yet. Not until we understand." |

**Bond**: Shared secret. Co-conspirators now.

---

#### Phase 7: Player Saves Thera
**"The Collapse"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Thera has been pushing too hard. The visions, the secrets, the constant demands. |
| **Scene** | Thera collapses. Actually falls. The healer who never stops, stopped. |
| **Key moment** | Player must care for Thera. Get them somewhere safe. Protect them from well-meaning others who would just need more. |
| **Reversal** | Thera wakes, disoriented. "I never... this doesn't happen to me." |

**What changes**: Player has proven they'll protect Thera, not just take from them.

---

#### Phase 8: Quiet Moment
**"The Small Grove"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Thera is recovering. They take the player to a hidden spot - a tiny clearing they've never shown anyone. |
| **Scene** | Just... being. No crisis. No demands. Sunlight through leaves. |
| **Key moment** | Thera laughs at something small. A real laugh, surprised out of them. Then goes quiet, almost embarrassed. |
| **What's said** | Maybe nothing important. Maybe everything. |

**What this is**: Intimacy. Not romantic necessarily - but real connection. Two people, present.

---

#### Phase 9: Thera's Dream
**"What I Would Want"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | Night. The Grove is quiet. They're talking, maybe couldn't sleep. |
| **Scene** | The player asks what Thera wants. For themselves. Not for others. |
| **The dream** | "A place like this. But... mine. Quiet mornings. Someone who stays. Time to just... be." |
| **Key moment** | Thera has never said this out loud. Maybe never let themselves think it. |
| **Aftermath** | Vulnerability. They've shown something real. |

**Why this matters**: When the sacrifice comes, we know exactly what they're giving up.

---

#### Phase 10: The Truth Together
**"The Roots Speak"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | The crisis is escalating. They need real answers. Thera's visions are screaming. |
| **Scene** | They go to the Deep, or the Roots. Thera listens in a way they've never let themselves. |
| **Revelation** | The Grove is a ship. They're arriving. Something must be given or everything dies. |
| **Key moment** | The weight of it lands. Thera goes pale. "I always knew. I didn't want to know." |

**Bond**: They face the truth together. Neither has to carry it alone.

#### Phase 11: The Choice
**"It Has to Be Me"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Setup** | The Elders confirm. A Heart is needed. A soul, willing, to become the ship's core for landing. |
| **Scene** | Thera understands. Their whole life - the giving, the feeling of purpose larger than themselves - led here. |
| **The player's attempt** | Can the player volunteer instead? (No. The ship chooses. It has to be a Rootspeaker. It has to be Thera.) |
| **Thera's response** | "You've given me more than anyone ever has. Let me give something too." |
| **Key moment** | This is not resignation. This is choice. Thera, for once, chooses. |

**The heartbreak begins**: We see it coming. We can't stop it.

**"The Heart"**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|

**What remains**: The hometown feels different now. Warm. Like someone is watching over it. Because someone is.

### The Companion: Thera

**The Warm Flame** - the healer everyone goes to, beloved heart of the community.

| Aspect | Answer |
|--------|--------|
| **Name** | **Thera** (working name - can adjust) |
| **Age** | Early 30s - fully formed, knows who they are |
| **Role** | Rootspeaker, the healer everyone trusts |
| **Position** | Central to the Heartwood - everyone knows them |

### Personality

| Trait | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Energy** | Warm, present, makes you feel seen immediately |
| **Voice** | Direct, honest, occasional fierce protectiveness |
| **Presence** | The person who remembers your name, asks how you're doing, means it |
| **Hidden side** | Quietly fierce - protects what they love without hesitation |

**How they make you feel**: Safe. Seen. Like you matter. They have a gift for full attention - when they're with you, they're WITH you.

### The Flaw

**They give too much.**

- They can't say no
- They can't rest
- They can't put themselves first
- They're slowly burning out but won't admit it
- Others take and take; Thera keeps giving
- The player sees this before Thera does

**How this shows up**:
- Interrupted meals (someone always needs them)
- Exhaustion they hide with practiced ease
- A moment where they snap, then immediately apologize
- The player catches them alone, finally still, looking lost

### The Dream

**A quieter life.**

- A small grove of their own
- Maybe someone to share it with
- Time to just... be
- Mornings that aren't interrupted
- Space to breathe

*They've never said this out loud. They might not even admit it to themselves.*

**Why this dream is devastating**: They'll never get it. The sacrifice takes it all away. And they know it when they choose.

### Why They Bond with the Player

**Initial**: They see the player is lost, new, needs care. They can't help but help.

**Evolution**: The player is different. Most people take from Thera. The player:
- Notices when Thera is tired
- Tells them to rest
- Gives back, not just takes
- Sees THEM, not just what they can do

**The shift**: This isn't just someone who needs them. This is an equal. Maybe a friend. Maybe more. Someone who cares about Thera as a person, not just a healer.

### The Sacrifice

**Why Thera chooses this**:
- Of course they would give everything - they always have
- But this time it's THEIR CHOICE
- Not obligation. Not because no one else will.
- Because they've finally been loved enough to know their life has value
- And they choose to give it anyway

**The difference**: They could have burned out, given everything by default, died emptied. Instead, they're choosing, eyes open, for people who finally showed them what receiving love feels like.

**Final words** (rough direction):
- To the player: acknowledgment of what they gave Thera
- About the dream: "I think... I found it. Not the grove. But the feeling."
- About the future: "Take care of them. Take care of yourself."

### Visual/Sensory Notes

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Appearance** | Practical clothes, worn soft. Always has herb-stained fingers. |
| **Sound** | Voice that calms. Hums while working. Laugh that surprises people. |
| **Habits** | Touches people gently when speaking. Remembers small details. |
| **Tell** | When truly exhausted, goes very still and quiet. |

### The Sacrifice Beat

**Setup Requirements:**
- All 12 phases should be completed (or most)
- Player has had multiple "I care about this person" moments
- Companion has expressed dreams/hopes for the future (making loss felt)
- The necessity of the sacrifice is established clearly

**The Moment Itself:**
- Must feel like THEIR choice, not forced by plot
- Player should have a chance to object, try alternatives
- Companion acknowledges the journey together
- References specific earlier moments (callbacks)
- Final words are meaningful, not rushed
- Player cannot stop it (or can try but fail)
- Silence after - let it land

**Aftermath:**
- Other NPCs react to the loss
- The hometown/ship feels different - alive with their presence
- Small reminders appear (a flower they liked, a phrase they used)
- The grief is honored, not rushed past

# 10. Session Notes

## 2026-01-31 - Session 1

**Key Decisions:**
- Monastery was placeholder, starting fresh
- Focus on ONE world first
- No content lockout (always-on MMO)
- Want to "draw them in" emotionally

**The Concept:**
- Players start in what appears to be a forest
- Gradually discover they're inside a giant seedship
- The mission: reseed a dying planet
- "Space druids" vibe without calling them druids

**The Companion Arc (Major Story Beat):**
- A companion character the player builds genuine rapport with
- At the climax, companion sacrifices their "heart essence" to stabilize the ship's landing
- Companion's soul imbues the hometown - it becomes a living, ensouled place
- LATE GAME REVEAL: The companion's soul is actually seeding the entire planet
- This is a path of soul evolution - becoming a sentient planetary consciousness
- The sacrifice transforms from tragedy → cosmic transcendence

**Cosmology Implications:**
- Souls can evolve into planetary beings
- The seedships aren't just carrying plants - they carry souls that will become planets
- Every planet may have started with a sacrifice like this
- The player's work terraforming/building is nurturing their friend's evolution

**Open Questions to Resolve:**
1. What do these people call themselves?
2. Do they know they're on a ship? (All? Some? None?)
3. What draws the player in initially? (The crisis pre-landing)
4. How long has the journey been?
5. What is the player's origin/role?
6. Who is the companion? (Most important NPC to design)

**Tone Direction:**
- Draw players in emotionally
- Mystery that unfolds naturally
- Stakes that feel personal AND cosmic
- Nature + technology blend (not fantasy, not hard sci-fi)
- Grief that transforms into wonder

**Reference Material: Alchemy of Souls (Korean Drama)**
- Teacher-student relationship that evolves into deep bond
- Key pattern: role inversion (servant who is actually the teacher)
- Brutal but effective mentorship, mutual vulnerability
- Multiple crisis moments where each saves the other
- Light & shadow symbolism - they complete each other
- The bond is built through: conflict → respect → trust → love
- Sacrifice feels earned because the relationship is earned
