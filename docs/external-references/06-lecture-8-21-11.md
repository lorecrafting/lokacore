# Dong Han Medical Lecture - August 21, 2011

## Overview

This lecture focuses on pulse textures, the Six Qi energies in detail, combined pulse interpretations, and the philosophical foundations of diagnosis.

---

## I. Core Philosophy of Diagnosis

### The Primacy of Diagnosis Over Treatment
- The secret of medicine is not in formulas but in correct diagnosis
- Most practitioners fail because they don't know what they're dealing with
- When helping students troubleshoot, the main job is identifying the problem

### Diagnostic Challenges by Patient Type
- Women: traditionally 10 times harder to diagnose
- Children: said to be harder but actually easier if you know how
- Older patients: hardest due to many layers of problems

### Mind-Body Connection
- Digestion of the same food differs based on emotional state
- Physical symptoms often have emotional/psychological roots
- Psychosomatic conditions often operate below conscious awareness

## II. The Secrecy of Pulse Reading

### Historical Context
- Pulse reading was "top secret in entire oriental medicine"
- Traditional teaching was one-to-one, requiring oaths of secrecy
- Written as reminders for those who already knew
- Teacher-to-student transmission was the norm

### Fundamental Anatomy
**Yin organs** (deep, close to bone):
- Heart, liver, spleen, lungs, kidneys
- Process vital functions constantly
- Store cellular memory and perception

**Yang organs** (superficial, close to skin):
- Hollow organs that process external material
- Temporary needs (hunger, thirst)

## III. The Three Sectors: Head, Body, and Tail

### Old Daoist Pulse System
- **Head**: Beginning of pulse wave; what is received/intake
- **Body**: Central vibration of organ's own function
- **Tail**: Ending of pulse wave; what is transmitted/output

### Direction Detection
- Determined by which way the wave appears to move
- Perfect pulse has no distinct head or tail
- Just smooth sine curve moving in direction of blood flow

### Nine Sectors (3x3 Matrix)
- Three positions (Cun, Guan, Chi) x Three depths
- Upper/middle/lower correspond to superficial, middle, and deep pressures

### Twenty-Seven Sectors
- Advanced level taught after students demonstrate competency
- Taught only to those who pass diagnostic tests

## IV. Pulse Qualities and Textures

### The Six Qi Energies (Liu Qi)
Korean: 풍서습조한열 (Wind, Warm, Damp, Dry, Cold, Hot)

| Energy | Pulse Quality |
|--------|---------------|
| Wind (풍) | Floating, loose, or tight |
| Warm (서) | Loose and weak |
| Dampness (습) | Fine velvet texture |
| Dryness (조) | Nothing there, like glass |
| Heat (열) | Sharp, round, needley |
| Cold (한) | Thin, tight, might disconnect |

### Basic Pulse Categories

**By Position/Depth:**
- **Floating (Fu)**: Superficial, light penetration
- **Deep (Chen)**: Deeper penetration, more severe

**By Quality:**
- **Urgent (Ji)**: Tight, thin, about to cut; cold attack
- **Slippery (Hua)**: Wet slippery vs hard slippery (distinguish!)
- **Scattered (San)**: All over, no clear line; danger of death
- **Straw/Green Onion**: Hollow inside; anemia or blood deficiency
- **Vast (Hong)**: Big and tall; extreme fever
- **Rolling/Beading**: Heat-containing wave

## V. Combined Pulse Interpretations

### Four Winds (Si Feng)
1. Wind-Wind: Floating and loose/slow
2. Cold-Wind: Tight and urgent
3. Warm-Wind: Loose and weak
4. Moist-Wind: Velvet-like sensation

### Floating Pulse Combinations

| Combination | Interpretation |
|-------------|----------------|
| Floating + Urgent | Cold attacking exterior; excessive |
| Floating + Weak | Deficient exterior |
| Floating + Fast + Weak | Deficiency outside with heat |
| Floating + Slow + Weak | Deficiency inside |
| Floating + Loose | Wind with dampness; swelling |
| Floating + Extremely Weak | Severe heat damage |
| Floating + Straw | Interior emptiness; blood deficiency |
| Floating + Big | Strong wind damage; skin infection |
| Floating + Slippery | Heat separated; digestive cold with skin heat |
| Floating + Vast | Extreme fever causing confusion |
| Floating + Scattered | Dying from vital force exhaustion |

### Position-Specific Interpretations

**Right Side:**
- Cun (Lung/LI): Upper respiratory, allergies, bowel
- Guan (Spleen/Stomach): Digestive distension, appetite loss
- Chi (Mingmen/Sanjiao): Bladder, nervous system, endocrine

**Left Side:**
- Cun (Heart/SI): Emotional effects, valve/muscle, nutrition
- Guan (Liver/GB): Spastic conditions, vision, migraine, joints
- Chi (Kidney/Bladder): Urination, fluid retention

## IX. Diagnostic Philosophy

### "What is happening" vs. "What it is"
Static diagnosis is useless. Must determine:
- Where energy is going
- What became what
- What is becoming what
- What happened before
- What will happen next

### Don't Project Expectations
- When you know patient information, you tend to look for expected signals
- Stay ignorant; receive and perceive without expectation
- "Draw what you see, not what you think you should see"

### The Scholar's Attitude
- A scholar is someone who is trying to learn
- Most proud moment: admitting "I don't know"
- Not knowing opens opportunity for knowledge

## X. Practical Technique

### Proper Pulse-Taking Mechanics
- Press evenly to the bone, then lift together
- All three fingers must apply equal pressure
- Chase the wave: follow it coming and going

### Depth Control
- Deeper pressure allows better sensing of downward movement
- Full finger area should sense, not just fingertip center

### Mental Preparation
- Meditate for about one hour before seeing patients
- Clear all life issues from mind
- Visualize holding the universe, zoom in to one patient
- Mind must be present in the wave
