# Dong Han Medical Lecture - July 16, 2011

## Overview

This lecture focuses on diagnostic methods with emphasis on pulse diagnosis, including the nine sector system, twelve basic pulses, cancer detection, and infant diagnostics.

---

## I. Foundational Principles of Diagnosis

### Mental Preparation
1. **Clear Mind** - Approach without assumptions or reliance on other doctors' opinions
2. **Independence** - Once you hear another doctor's diagnosis, there's tendency to follow it
3. **Build from the Ground Up** - Start with clear data; come to your own conclusions
4. **Patient Subjectivity Warning** - Patients are in suffering state, making observations subjective

### Deductive Diagnosis
- In medicine, we either "confirm or rule out"
- A positive confirmation or negative exclusion process

### Translation to Modern Terminology
- Without physiology/pathology knowledge, observations cannot be translated
- Five Elements/Six Energy terms don't communicate specific diagnoses
- Must translate to modern pathology terms

**Recommended Study:**
- Pathology textbook
- Physiology textbook
- Gray's Anatomy
- Textbook of Internal Medicine

## II. Diagnostic Modalities (In Order)

### 1. Aura/Energy Field Reading
- Reading the energy field around the patient
- Shows current condition, not fundamental underlying issues

### 2. Skin Surface Observation
**Facial Diagnosis:**
- Different areas correspond to different organs
- Most detailed expression found in tongue and eyes

**Color Assessment:**
- Must determine patient's NORMAL color first
- Looking for deviation from personal normal

**Texture Assessment:**
- **Shiny (sheen)**: Good body fluid, collagen, circulation
- **Matte/Flat**: Disease - malnutrition, poor circulation

**Location Importance:**
- Stomach area most important for chronic/life-threatening conditions
- Contains most meridians and channels

### 3. Five Sensory Organs (Five Element Correspondence)
| Sensory Organ | Element | Related Organ |
|--------------|---------|---------------|
| Eyes | Wood | Liver |

| Lips/Mouth | Earth | Stomach/Spleen |

### 4. Listening
- Not the content but the voice quality
- Strength, texture, way of speaking, breath, length of pronunciation

### 5. Questioning
- Pain, history, food intake, appetite, sleeping patterns, bowel movements
- Remember patient input is subjective

### 6. Palpation/Sensing/Touching
- Spinal condition, abdominal condition, limbs
- **Pulse diagnosis** (most important)

## III. Infant Diagnostics

### Index Finger Examination (San Jian / Bokak)
**Location:** Under index finger, between metacarpal bones on right side

**What to Look For:** Thin white line that rises when infantile conditions develop

**Severity Scale:**
- Line reaches first joint: Mild condition
- Line reaches second joint: Severe condition
- Line reaches third joint: Very difficult to revive (near death)

**Condition Indicated:** Infantile jaundice - acute liver failure

### Infant Pulse Assessment
- **Normal Ratio:** 5 pulses per 1 breath
- **Abnormal:** More than 5 (6, 7, 8) = Too much heat - life-threatening

### Infant Skin Conditions
- **Most Common (9/10 cases):** Fungal/yeast infection from mother during delivery
- **Traditional Treatment:** Burn moxa and use the smoke; add sulfur for severe cases

## IV. Pulse Diagnosis Fundamentals

### Basic Pulse Wave
- **Normal Pulse:** Perfect sine curve - completely balanced yin and yang
- **"Spleen pulse"** = balanced, centered pulse (not literally spleen)

### Four Season Pulses
- Summer pulse does NOT mean normal pulse in summer
- Summer pulse = pulse affected by heat-like energy
- Winter pulse = pulse affected by cold-like energy

### The Twelve Basic Pulses

| Pulse Type | Description | Indication |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Superficial | Movement only on surface | Position, not depth |
| Deep | Movement underneath | Position only |
| Slack | Slower than 5 per breath | Cold condition |
| Rapid | More than 5 per breath | Carrying heat |
| Slippery | Oily, rolls | Hyperactivity, abundant secretion |
| Sandy | Matte, like wet sand | Functional atrophy |
| Long | High wave amplitude | Tall |
| Short | Low wave amplitude | Short |
| Large | Wide pulse | Capacity/reservoir |
| Small | Narrow pulse | Limited capacity |
| Knotted | Touches back on itself | Cancer |
| Looping | Circular without completing | Cyst or tumor |

### Pulse Dimensions
1. **Height**: Energy affecting/penetrating other organs
2. **Width (Large/Small)**: Capacity - how much damage COULD be done
3. **Texture**: Quality (slippery, sandy, etc.)

### Five Depth Levels (from center line)
- Upward: Lung → Heart → Spleen → Liver → Kidney
- Above Lung level = Brain
- Below Kidney level = Bone marrow and Brain

## V. Cancer/Tumor Detection

### Looping Pulse
- Incomplete circle = Early stage (possibly Stage 0)
- Complete loop = Cyst or tumor

### Knotted Pulse
- Pulse touches itself = Cancer
- The more complete the knot, the more advanced

### Cancer Stages in Pulse
- **Stage 0:** Nodules, inflammation, mutated tissue
- **Stage 1:** Early cancer development
- **Stage 2:** Affecting lymphatic system/other organs
- **Stage 3:** Fully matured cancer, potentially metastatic
- **Stage 4:** Three or more organs affected

### Infection Detection
- **Small Round Texture:** Bacterial infection
- **Small Sandy Texture:** Viral infection

## X. Specific Conditions and Pulses

### Hypertension
- Deep pulse that gets stronger when pressed
- Shows in heart position

### Kidney Failure
- Sandy pulse with "background noise" shooting between beats
- Feels like electronic zapping (zzzzz)
- Precedes blood test abnormalities by ~1 week

### Stroke/Paralysis Indicators
- String pulse with extreme tension
- Goes beyond 5th level (into brain territory)
- Wind entering nervous system

### Appendicitis vs Gas Pain
- Diagnostic test: Flex knee, press ileocecal area, release suddenly
- Patient jumps = appendicitis
- Need white blood cell count to confirm
