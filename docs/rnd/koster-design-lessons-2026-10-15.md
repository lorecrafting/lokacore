# Koster's worlds teach: players break secrets

Raph Koster's central principle across three decades is that **a persistent world is a society you govern, not a game you ship**: "I design places and worlds and societies, and not just games" ([Still Logged In](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=30637)). Four lessons recur across LegendMUD, Ultima Online (UO), Star Wars Galaxies (SWG), Metaplace and Stars Reach. First, **players decode every formula and automate every chore** ([Use-based systems](https://www.raphkoster.com/2006/07/18/use-based-systems/); [Laws](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/)). Second, **every faucet of money needs a drain, designed in advance** ([UO economy](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/snippets/the-evolution-of-uos-economy/)). Third, **players do what the game rewards** ([SWG part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)). Fourth, **convenience features can erase the emergent play a world was built for** ([auction house post](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=4080)). His most-cited mistakes mostly show players defeating a hidden or optimizable system: UO's failed player self-policing, SWG's secret Jedi unlock that hint items exposed, and SWG's server-wide market emptying player shops ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/a-brief-history-of-murder-in-ultima-online); [A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/)). Loka plans hidden elite professions, numbers held in cartridges on the player's phone, and a later move online. Its biggest risk is the Jedi failure in a new form: data the client holds becomes public, and an offline save the player controls cannot be trusted by a shared economy ([Laws](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/)). A correction to the brief: Koster was promoted off the SWG team to SOE Chief Creative Officer before the NGE (15 Nov 2005) and **left SOE on 24 March 2006, four months after it** ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/raph-koster-leaves-sony-online-entertainment); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raph_Koster)).

*Method and uncertainty marks.* Most raphkoster.com text reached the researchers through a summarizing fetch tool, so quotes should be spot-checked against the originals. Tags used below: *(snippet only)* means the claim rests on a search-result snippet; *(search summary)* means a search tool relayed it; *(secondary)* means the source is not Koster; *(disputed)* means sources conflict; *(emulator)* means fan emulator data, not original SWG. The *Postmortems* book text, the *A Theory of Fun* book text, the Trust Spectrum talk video and the GDC Vault talks were not read.

## Fun is learning, and systems outlast story

Koster defines fun narrowly as "the positive feedback given by the brain for cognitive learning," which he calls "kfun." He separates it from aesthetic delight, visceral thrills and social status, which can travel alongside it but are not its core ([Sorts of fun](https://www.raphkoster.com/2005/11/01/sorts-of-fun-sorts-of-kfun/)). The often-quoted short form, fun as "the act of mastering a problem mentally," and the claim that boredom means a pattern has been "grokked" come from secondary summaries of the book *(snippet only)* ([Game Studies Wiki](https://game-studies.fandom.com/wiki/A_Theory_of_Fun_for_Game_Design?oldid=4074)). Critics such as Ian Bogost reject fun as the main purpose of games *(search summary)* (same source). His 2025 essay "Game design is simple, actually" restates the theory as working steps. **Fun is "making progress on prediction."** Games are problems made of nested operational loops and progression spirals. Feedback must show "what you can do, that you did it, what difference it made, and whether it helped" ([Game design is simple](https://www.raphkoster.com/2025/11/03/game-design-is-simple-actually/)). His "grammar of gameplay" splits games into mechanics, content, metaphor and narrative, with verbs as the atoms: "chess pieces aren't content, they are verbs" *(search summary)*. Who coined "ludeme" is disputed ([grammar slides](https://www.raphkoster.com/gaming/atof/grammarofgameplay.pdf); [announcement](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=487)).

That framework explains his position on narrative. **"Narrative in a game is not a mechanic. It's a form of a feedback."** Authored narrative is "destined to be expensive, short, and over," and "if the systems of your game are outweighed by the feedback, you should grow suspicious" ([Narrative is not a game mechanic](https://www.raphkoster.com/2012/01/20/narrative-is-not-a-game-mechanic/)). PopMatters counters that he treats narrative as static information *(snippet only)* ([PopMatters](https://popmatters.com/153895-narrative-is-a-game-mechanic-2495891347.html)). No Koster text was found that names "emergent story" as an explicit policy. His economics backs the position: his 2018 cost analysis argues for moving from authored "bytes" toward community and systemic content ([GamesBrief](https://www.gamesbrief.com/2018/01/raph-koster-on-the-cost-of-games-and-its-not-pretty/)). The SWG team found that hand-built content "could not keep pace" with players, while procedural generation made the world feel alive: "we shouldn't necessarily know what was in our world" ([SWG's Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/)).

The *Laws of Online World Design* are mostly aphorisms Koster collected from the MUD-Dev list. Only "some of the laws are indeed my own," and most attribution is lost ([Laws intro](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/)). "Koster's Law," that roleplay quality falls as player count rises, was named by Mike Sellers, not by Koster. **Koster's Theorem** is his: "Virtual social bonds evolve from the fictional towards real social bonds." The list also holds "The client is in the hands of the enemy," "If something can be abused, it will be," "someone is going to automate the process of playing your world," the claim that "players will expect all labor to result in profit," a warning that without drains you get "Monty Haul syndrome," the 250-person ideal community size, and the instruction to "expect to keep your team on it indefinitely" ([Laws list](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/)). Bartle's four player types appear on the companion Rules page as Bartle's own work, and the Schubert Triangle is Damion Schubert's ([Rules page](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-rules-of-online-world-design/)). The phrase "community is the content" was not found in these pages and should not be attributed to the Laws. His essays are collected in *Postmortems: Selected Essays Volume One* (2018), the first of a planned three, with a foreword by Richard Garriott. Its seven parts cover MUDs, UO (the resource system, player killing, the economy and "A Story About a Tree"), SWG (the postmortem series, a new NGE piece and "A Jedi Saga") and Metaplace, and he says it is "not a memoir or tell-all" ([Postmortems](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/books/postmortems/)). It runs 702 pages *(search summary)* ([Video Game Library](https://www.thevideogamelibrary.org/book/postmortems-selected-essays-vol-1)).

## LegendMUD and Ultima Online showed that players break every assumption

### LegendMUD made myth real and gated skills by hometown

LegendMUD, a DikuMUD derivative opened on 14 Feb 1994, set its zones in real places across history and treated **each age's beliefs as concrete reality**. An industrial-era San Francisco character could learn guns but not magic. An ancient Irish character could learn druidic skills but not field surgery ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LegendMUD)). Koster says he did not create it alone. Sherry Menton devised the theme and was an original implementor with Rick Delashmit. Raph and Kristen Koster joined before launch, wrote a large share of the content and later took over the game ([Stratics interview](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=22536)). Bartle's credit to Koster as design lead is contested ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LegendMUD)). Hometown chose which skills a player could reach, inside a classless hybrid with levels. Skills came from NPC teachers, sometimes after small quests ([Stratics](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=22536); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LegendMUD)). He credits its variety (baseball, Shakespeare at the Globe, WWI trenches) to broad themes, easy tools and cheap text content. He also warns that **MUD features break at MMO scale**: announcing every quest badge to everyone would be "intolerable spam" ([Stratics](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=22536)). The claim that LegendMUD's classless design "shaped UO powerfully" rests on Wikipedia wording, not his own *(snippet only)* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LegendMUD)).

### UO's use-based skills taught that formulas get decoded

UO opened all skills to everyone. Each use gave a chance to rise, inside a **700-point total pool**, and gains at the cap took points from another skill. There was no difficulty rating, so trivial actions advanced skills, and early on players could even advance by watching others. Use rates differed widely, dynamic rate scaling failed for lack of a baseline, and players macroed for gain rolls that fell to about one in ten thousand. The RNG was seeded predictably, which produced the "8x8 macroing" exploit ([Use-based systems](https://www.raphkoster.com/2006/07/18/use-based-systems/)). His lessons: **"No matter what you do, players will decode every formula, statistic, and algorithm in your world via experimentation."** Practice without challenge bores. Costless failure lets players "bottomfeed" on easy actions. Fix the feedback loop, make skills enable other play rather than be the goal, and track usage rates (same source). In 2019 he added that the system ignored social play such as running roleplay taverns, and that SWG was built to reward it ([Game Developer 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/welcome-back-koster-why-an-mmorpg-vet-is-back-building-a-new-virtual-world)).

### UO's economy, ecology and housing ran out of balance

Spawns minted gold, drains were too few, and **equilibrium was never designed**. NPC shops that bought speculatively became "a gold spigot" that rewarded macroing. "Every single crafter in the game went into the hole immediately, because they gladly traded gold for skill points." Gold held at about 1 USD to 200 gold for a while, then lost about 100x after Renaissance (his estimate). Hardcoded currency values in critter rewards and castle prices made fixes hard ([UO economy](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/snippets/the-evolution-of-uos-economy/)). He also says "we actually had to take OUT realistic economy sim aspects because they weren't fun" and "Every tiny change sent massive tremors though the entire game" ([UO economy page](https://raphkoster.com/gaming/economy.shtml)). Zach Simpson's 1999 analysis popularized the "faucet-drain" framing ([Koster on ecology](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=46439)). Bugs became the beloved rares minigame: "Far more often than you would think, you get magic through serendipity" ([UO rares](https://www.raphkoster.com/2010/11/24/how-uo-rares-were-born/)).

The planned ecology gave creatures food, shelter and desire needs and drove spawns from resource surplus ([resource system part 1](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=517); [part 2](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=519)). **The cause of its failure is disputed.** Koster says the AI was disabled for CPU cost after a beta rewrite "by an engineer who didn't really like or understand it." He says the real collapse came from the economy loop: players crafted wool shirts to grind skill, and the central bank ran out of wool ([Did players destroy the UO ecology?](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=46439)). The rival version, that players killed prey and predators faster than the system rebalanced, comes from a 2004 blog *(secondary; does not name Koster)* ([Tauber](https://jtauber.com/blog/2004/03/13/free_markets_and_ecosystem_simulation/)). Garriott warns against simulated ecology, while Koster still wants to try ([Ultima Codex](https://ultimacodex.com/2017/10/raph-koster-on-ultima-onlines-influence/)). On housing, primary Koster material is thin. The Laws say "ownership is key," and house decay acted as a drain ([Laws](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/); [UO economy](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/snippets/the-evolution-of-uos-economy/)). Malas added land because scarce land drove price inflation *(snippet only)* ([UO expansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_Online_expansions)).

### UO's player-killing ended the dream of self-policing

Guards, color names, bounties and the Spring 1998 reputation system all failed to stop player killing. Koster admits that "people would do things, lose notoriety, and not know why," and that "In the name of player freedoms, I had put them through a slow-drip torture of two years of experiments" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/a-brief-history-of-murder-in-ultima-online)). The Trammel/Felucca split **doubled the userbase**. He "wouldn't have done it, personally," and says it "came at the cost of a lot of magical stuff" and that "the dream of letting players police themselves was over" (same; [Making of UO](https://www.raphkoster.com/2012/02/17/making-of-uo-articles-at-mmorpg-com/)). He opposed the switch as "trading away player self-determination for security" and believed player-run cities controlling PvP could have worked ([Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/raph-koster-on-fire/)). Gordon Walton's claim that PK drove off 70% of new players is *(snippet only)* ([Wikipedia: UO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_Online)). His overall verdict: "many of the things that were tried in UO didn't work," systems "broke under unforeseen player stresses," "many were simply bad ideas," and "we were young and stupid and didn't realize we were building something impossible" ([Ultima Codex](https://ultimacodex.com/2017/10/raph-koster-on-ultima-onlines-influence/); [Ten Lessons](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/presentations/ten-lessons-learned-2/)).

## Star Wars Galaxies built interdependence, then a secret broke it

### Pre-CU professions were a skill-box tree with a capacity budget

At launch SWG had **34 professions: 6 basic and 24 advanced**. Each was a tree of Novice, four branches of four boxes, and Master ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)). Koster describes "over 30 professions, each with 18 skills," with a cap set to favor hybrid builds over deep specialists ([SWG part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)). **The budget figure is disputed.** A fan wiki says 250 skill points ([skill points wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Skill_points)). The official manual calls it "Learning Capacity" and says players can master three professions and "dabble in a fourth" ([manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)). XP was typed by activity: "Nearly everything you do earns you some form of XP." Players spent it at trainers and could teach each other through apprenticeship. Surrender freed capacity but refunded neither XP nor credits (same manual). Elite professions unlocked from specific boxes or masteries: Doctor needed Master Medic, and Bounty Hunter needed Master Marksman plus Master Scout. Politician cost no skill points, which kept the civic role separate from the combat build (same manual; [Politician wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Politician)). Koster's aim was "a type of XP for every major activity" and horizontal power through new capabilities ([part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)). Wikipedia says he later called the profession approach a mistake because few players became generalists, but his post does not confirm this *(unconfirmed)* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)).

### Crafting and the social web made professions need each other

SWG had **474 resource types in 79 subclasses**, randomly rolled and placed by Perlin noise. Mined-out types vanished permanently. Items decayed and needed repair, and factories produced only while the right resources lasted ([part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)). Experimentation traded points against failure risk, and resource quality capped results ([Experimentation 101](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_035.html)). Crafters earned XP when others used their goods ([Kebernet guide](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_039.html)), although Koster says a planned version was cut for database load *(disputed: a form shipped)* ([part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)). Resource attribute lists (OQ, CD, CR and so on) come from SWG Restoration *(emulator)* ([SWGR](https://swgr.org/wiki/crafting_resources/)). Koster suggests a resource half-life would have cleared database bloat ([Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/)). All shops were player-run, NPC drops were mostly junk, and wealth followed a Pareto curve ([part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)). Missions minted most money, and "The sink is basically the whole game" ([Astromech economy stats](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/essays/astromech-stats-economy-stats/)). Combat caused wounds and battle fatigue that only doctors and entertainers could cure, which tied fighters to cantinas, chefs and hospitals. He admits **the fatigue loop never worked because the game could not tell session length**, that buffs reached about 400% against a modest target, and that "We never paper gamed combat" ([part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)). His two sharpest rules come from this work: **"players do, and respect, what the game rewards"** and **"a content-dependent profession is a terrible idea in an MMO"** (same).

### Cities and housing shaped the map

Players founded cities by placing a City Hall and gathering declared residents. Rank unlocked cantinas, hospitals and shuttleports. Weekly maintenance, decay of unpaid structures and three-weekly mayoral elections kept cities under pressure. The archived wiki page is a 2011 version, so the pre-CU rank table is unverified ([Player city wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Player_city)). Free placement under exclusion rules around core cities produced an NPC city, an empty ring and an outer "crust" of player towns, and it left shops hard to find ([Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/)). Pets, cities and housing existed to tie players to the world ([part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)).

### The Jedi secret leaked, then live ops finished the job

Jedi were "an alpha class" that everyone wanted, rare in the films and powerful. Koster's ideal was a locked permadeath slot hunted by bounty hunters and finally Vader. The answer he got was "Permadeath?!?" ([A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-brilliant-star-wars-galaxies-idea-that-didnt-happen-1699219345)). Wikipedia instead says permadeath was planned and then removed at SOE's request *(disputed)* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)). What shipped was "absolutely security through obscurity": a secret randomized list, which crunch shrank to mastering hidden professions, "a very fateful decision made at the end of a 14- or 16-hour crunch day" *(search summary; verify)* ([A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/star-wars-galaxies-devs-discuss-why-it-died)). LucasArts marketing wanted a Jedi by Christmas, so Holocrons gave hints. **"As soon as three people all have gotten a hint that what they need is to master a specific skill box, the secret was out."** "Everyone started playing everything they didn't like," and "After Holocrons, the game was dead; it was just that nobody knew it yet" ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2015/04/20/how-the-desire-for-jedi-killed-star-wars-galaxies/)). The first player Jedi appeared on 7 Nov 2003 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)). Publish 9 replaced the hidden list with a visible Force Sensitive village path *(player guide)* ([Jedi Path guide](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070127174504/index.html)). Koster says it did not stop grinders. He ends: "I never played a Jedi at all" ([A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/)).

**CU (early 2005)** replaced queued combat and pruned professions because 30+ trees were hard to balance *(secondary)* ([MMO Folklorist](https://mmofolklorist.com/2023/01/05/star-wars-galaxies-a-brief-history/)). **The NGE (15 Nov 2005)**, announced with about two weeks' notice, swapped skill boxes for nine classes and action combat ([Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/swg-nge-crying-freeman/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)). Smedley, in a deleted forum post relayed by a blog *(secondary)*, said "we knew we would never be able to attract enough people" with the old game ([Edery.org](https://edery.org/?p=67)). Koster argues that **"the bulk of the problems over time resulted from Live operations."** An August 2004 group-XP change "doubled the churn rate," and churn stayed 50% higher afterward. The team chose the wrong metric when it judged an auction-house change by counting Master Merchants instead of shop owners, and it changed things while "flying in the fog with bad instruments." He also admits "I didn't really train a replacement" when he moved up to CCO ([Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/)). His statement that he was not responsible for the NGE is an interviewer's paraphrase ([Escapist interview](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=17852)). The server-wide market created a "perfect information economy": "When the merchant changes went in to SWG, the merchants went out," because **"every inconvenience is potentially someone's game."** At peak, about half of players ran a shop ([auction house post](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=4080)).

## Governing the society: rights, social mechanics and trust

The 2000 **Declaration of the Rights of Avatars** follows the 1789 French declaration. It opens "Avatars are the manifestation of actual people in an online medium," and asks for a published code of conduct before any punishment, proportional penalties, presumption of innocence, revocable powers for enforcers, fees known in advance, and the protection of data integrity, persistence and privacy. He calls it "a hypothetical exercise," yet Metaplace's 2008 terms were modelled on it ([Declaration](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=16044)). "Still Logged In" (GDC 2017) turns this into operator duties. **"If you host an online community where people can cause harm to another person, you are on the hook."** Keep a rolling log of recent interactions for disputes. Make identity persistent so bans mean something. "Block and mute have never been adequate enough tools." **"Every feature you make should be looked at first as a weapon."** Limit and log staff powers, because UO game masters traded favors for items. Assume virtual goods have real value and plan for vulnerable users. "rendering is by far the least important part of this entire field" ([Still Logged In](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=30637)).

His social-mechanics talk (GDCO 2010) builds a ladder from helping, status and races up to rival goods, secrets, deception, roles, rituals and commons dilemmas. In it he defines single player as play "not... in opposition to, or in parallel with, or collaboratively with, someone else. (In other words, hardly anything.)" **Persistent competitive arenas "lead to the average player being a loser,"** and scale-free networks collapse when their hubs leave, as in SWG ([social mechanics slides](https://www.raphkoster.com/gaming/gdco2010/socialmechanics.pdf)). The **Trust Spectrum** (GDC 2018, with Aaron Cammarata of Google ATAP) has five rungs from no trust to intimate. Its design axes include asymmetry, resource pools, risk, information and time pressure. "Low trust is simply more accessible." Designers should avoid a "broken line" with nothing between low and high trust, and should not force intimacy ([Trust Spectrum](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=32038)). The talks "Moore's Wrath" and "Game Design is Hard" were not found under those titles. The nearest is a "Moore's Wall" deck claiming MMOs need about 400 hours of content against 8 to 12 for typical games *(search summary)* ([Moore's Wall PDF](https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Moores-Wall-IBM-Games-On-Demand.pdf)).

## Metaplace and Stars Reach: what he changed afterward

Metaplace (open 2007 to 2009, closed 1 Jan 2010) hosted user-built web worlds with Lua scripting and portable identity ([raphkoster.com 2013](https://raphkoster.com/2013/08)). The world count is disputed: "tens of thousands" by his account, about 70,000 per Hypergrid Business ([Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2009/12/metaplace-closing-a-warning-for-virtual-worlds-users/)). His diagnosis: **"we were too many things to too many different people."** Building was too hard, the behavior tools "probably didn't go far enough," it "got almost no marketing," and people stayed "perpetually confused as to what it was." Users had days to recover their work ([raphkoster.com 2013](https://raphkoster.com/2013/08); [Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2009/12/metaplace-closing-a-warning-for-virtual-worlds-users/)). His GDC 2008 thesis was "Put worlds on the web, not the web in a virtual world" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2008-02-25-gdc08-raph-kosters-reinventing-mmos-a-metaplace-antemortem.html)).

Stars Reach, from Playable Worlds (founded 2018), revisits SWG with stated fixes. It has 40+ combinable SWG-style professions, "the best gear is crafted," player cities and governments, and terraforming ([starsreach.com](https://www.starsreach.com)). Each cubic meter tracks humidity, temperature and structure *(search summary)* ([Massively OP 2024](https://massivelyop.com/2024/06/28/raph-kosters-new-mmorpg-is-called-stars-reach-and-yes-its-basically-star-wars-galaxies-2/)). The aim is a "wiki resistant" world, and the team does not intervene in the economy ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/stars-reach/raph-koster-interview-wiki-resistant)). His explicit fixes for SWG's monopolies: **"Getting big should increase your overhead,"** "scale should increase risk of collapse," and running a city should cost "a lot more" than a house. He also says "There isn't any such thing as balancing a chef against a rifleman" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/interview-mmo-pioneer-raph-koster-on-stars-reach-and-the-future-of-sandbox-game-design)). Kickstarter taught him to keep rewards cosmetic ("you don't want to be selling advantage") and to build community before launch: "You can't build [a community] on the fly" ([Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/raph-koster-playable-worlds-stars-reach-kickstarter)). The funding total is disputed: about $38M per Massively OP *(search summary)* against $45M per Game File ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2025/01/11/raph-kosters-stars-reach-mmorpg-is-launching-a-kickstarter-in-february/); [Game File](https://www.gamefile.news/p/stars-reach-interview-raph-koster-dirt-clouds-bunnies)). The Kickstarter raised about $740K by Kicktraq's count and "over $750K" in press reports ([Kicktraq](https://www.kicktraq.com/projects/starsreach/stars-reach/); [MMORPG.gg](https://mmorpg.gg/stars-reach-fully-funds-in-less-than-an-hour-on-kickstarter-new-mmorpg-from-raph-koster/)). ## Conclusion

Koster's catalogue of failures is less a list of bad ideas than a list of hidden assumptions that players exposed. UO assumed honest skill practice, SWG assumed a secret would stay secret, and both assumed a balance would hold while the operators changed it. His later work answers with structure rather than secrecy: visible paths, costs that scale with size, cosmetic-only monetization, and simulation deep enough that a wiki cannot flatten it. For Loka the sharpest point is that its architecture already decides some of these questions. Cartridge numbers that sit on the player's phone are a public wiki in waiting. An offline save the player controls is a client "in the hands of the enemy" the moment it touches a shared Realm ([Laws](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/)). Offline-first is also an advantage Koster never had. A single-player world has no griefers, no central bank to drain and no live-ops churn, so Loka can tune its economy and profession web with telemetry and controlled inputs before any of his multiplayer failure modes can appear.

## Checklist for Loka

Each item is a recommendation drawn from the cited lesson, not a claim Koster made about Loka.

### Profession skill tree and hidden elites
- [ ] Use typed XP per activity, so every major activity (crafting, trade, civic and social play) advances something ([SWG part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/); [Game Developer 2019](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/welcome-back-koster-why-an-mmorpg-vet-is-back-building-a-new-virtual-world)).
- [ ] Gate gains on difficulty and give failure a cost, so spamming trivial actions cannot "bottomfeed" ([Use-based systems](https://www.raphkoster.com/2006/07/18/use-based-systems/)).
- [ ] Hold a hard capacity budget that favors hybrids, and record an explicit decision on whether surrender refunds XP. SWG refunded capacity but not XP ([manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)).
- [ ] Treat hidden elite requirements as public. Cartridge data on the phone will be read, so give each elite a discoverable in-play path rather than relying on secrecy ([A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/); [Use-based systems](https://www.raphkoster.com/2006/07/18/use-based-systems/)).
- [ ] Never require mastering professions the player dislikes to unlock an elite ("Everyone started playing everything they didn't like") ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2015/04/20/how-the-desire-for-jedi-killed-star-wars-galaxies/)).
- [ ] Do not maximize both rarity and power for an elite everyone wants ([A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/)).
- [ ] Make every profession rest on systemic demand. None should depend on a stream of authored content ([SWG part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)).
- [ ] Paper-prototype combat and buff math before building it, and cap buff stacking ([SWG part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)).

### Crafting interdependence
- [ ] Pair each role with a good that others consume repeatedly, through decay, repair, food and medicine. Do not tie recovery loops to session length, which a phone game cannot predict ([SWG part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/); [part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)).
- [ ] Let resource attributes feed recipe stats through cartridge-defined weights, with an experimentation risk/reward choice ([Experimentation 101](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_035.html)).
- [ ] Give resources a half-life or rotation so old types expire cleanly ([Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/)).
- [ ] Never make skill-up crafting a guaranteed money loser, and cap NPC buyback so it cannot become a "gold spigot" ([UO economy](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/snippets/the-evolution-of-uos-economy/); [Laws](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/)).

### Economy
- [ ] Have the engine emit a faucet and sink ledger per source from day one, and pick the metric that measures the behavior you care about ([Astromech economy stats](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/essays/astromech-stats-economy-stats/); [Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/)).
- [ ] Keep every price and reward in cartridges, never hardcoded, so fixes do not need a wipe ([UO economy](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/snippets/the-evolution-of-uos-economy/)).
- [ ] Guarantee newcomers access to starter goods ([UO economy page](https://raphkoster.com/gaming/economy.shtml)).
- [ ] Do not ship a global perfect-information market early. Keep some friction that sustains player shops ([auction house post](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=4080)).

### Housing and towns
- [ ] Use upkeep, decay and civic roles outside the skill budget (the Politician pattern) ([Player city wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Player_city); [Politician wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Politician)).
- [ ] Make overhead and collapse risk grow with town size, so large players cannot lock up markets ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/interview-mmo-pioneer-raph-koster-on-stars-reach-and-the-future-of-sandbox-game-design)).
- [ ] Decide land supply and placement rules before the Realm opens, and keep shops findable ([Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/); [UO expansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_Online_expansions)).

### Offline-to-online transition and the Realm
- [ ] Write an import policy before the Realm exists. Offline currency, items and skills must not enter the shared economy unvetted, because the Realm server must be authoritative ([Laws](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/)).
- [ ] Decide PvP consent up front rather than bolting it on later ([Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/raph-koster-on-fire/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/a-brief-history-of-murder-in-ultima-online)).
- [ ] Review every Realm feature first as a weapon, and test with adversarial players ([Still Logged In](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=30637)).
- [ ] Publish a code of conduct and sanction ladder, keep persistent identity and a rolling interaction log, and log staff commands ([Still Logged In](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=30637); [Declaration](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=16044)).
- [ ] Guarantee save-data integrity and an export path for player-owned work ([Declaration](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=16044); [Hypergrid Business](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2009/12/metaplace-closing-a-warning-for-virtual-worlds-users/)).
- [ ] Stage social play along the trust ladder (market, then gifts and helping, then party crafts, then shared housing) with no gap between low and high trust ([Trust Spectrum](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=32038)).
- [ ] Avoid persistent power-law ladders that make the average player a loser, and do not let announcements spam everyone at scale ([social mechanics slides](https://www.raphkoster.com/gaming/gdco2010/socialmechanics.pdf); [Stratics](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=22536)).
- [ ] Plan a permanent live team for the Realm, and train a successor who holds the design vision ([Laws](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/laws-of-online-world-design/the-laws-of-online-world-design/); [Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/)).

### New world lore and cartridges
- [ ] Budget authored text as a consumable, and let systems carry replay ([Narrative is not a game mechanic](https://www.raphkoster.com/2012/01/20/narrative-is-not-a-game-mechanic/)).
- [ ] Consider LegendMUD's rule that each place's beliefs are literally true and gate skills by origin. It is a cheap text-native way to give identity without classes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LegendMUD); [Stratics](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=22536)).
- [ ] Keep the authoring tools for cartridges aimed at one audience and easy to use ([raphkoster.com 2013](https://raphkoster.com/2013/08)).
