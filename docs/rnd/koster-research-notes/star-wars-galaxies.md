# Star Wars Galaxies (SWG) design: launch (2003) through CU and NGE, and Raph Koster's lessons

Method note: Koster's own blog posts (April 2015 SWG postmortem series) were read via a summarising fetch tool, so quotes are as returned and should be spot-checked against the posts. Official pre-CU manual and Allakhazam guides were read from Internet Archive copies of swgemu.com mirror pages. SWG Fandom wiki pages were read via Internet Archive; some are late-era (2011) and not pre-CU. SWG Restoration (swgr.org) is a fan emulator, NOT original pre-CU; its numbers are excluded except where flagged.

## Professions and skill boxes at launch

### Takeaway
Launch SWG had 6 starting professions and ~24 advanced (elite/hybrid) ones, each a tree of Novice + 4 disciplines of 4 boxes + Master, bought with typed XP and capped by a fixed budget (250 skill points per wiki; "Learning Capacity" bar in the manual), with free surrender of skills to re-spec (you lose the XP and credits spent). Elite and hybrid professions unlock by finishing specific boxes or masters in other professions.

### Cited Findings
- 34 professions at launch: six basic (Artisan, Brawler, Entertainer, Marksman, Medic, Scout) and 24 advanced. Each is a tree: Novice, four branches of four levels, Master requiring all four branches. — [Wikipedia: SWG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)
- Koster: "over 30 professions, each with 18 skills" (not counting Jedi or space); only a limited number of abilities active at once; the cap was chosen to favor hybrid builds over deep specialization; at the cap you could surrender a skill to learn another. — [Koster, Designing a Living Society in SWG, part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)
- Budget: "Every player character in SWG has 250 skill points"; each profession = 4 branches of 4 boxes plus Novice and Master; box costs vary (Novice Marksman 15 pts, Medical Support III 4 pts, Master Chef 2 pts); surrendering returns the points; Jump to Lightspeed pilot professions cost no points. Page is tagged "Pre-NGE ... no longer accurate". — [SWG Wiki: Skill points (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Skill_points)
- The official manual does not say "250"; it calls the budget "Learning Capacity": a bar that fills as you learn; "all characters can gain complete mastery in three full professions and dabble in a fourth". Different skills have different capacity costs. — [Official SWG manual ch.5 (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html) (consistency of 250 points with "3 masters + dabble" not verified)
- Four components: XP, skills, titles, professions. "There are actually several different types of XP", each tied to an activity (Pistol XP for blaster kills, Unarmed Combat XP for fists); "Nearly everything you do earns you some form of XP." XP is spent at NPC trainers (usually in city Training Halls). Each skill has an XP cost and usually a prerequisite skill (Advanced Blaster Pistol needs Intermediate). — [Manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)
- Koster's design goal: "a type of XP for every major activity in the game" so non-combat play is recognised. A planned crafting XP that rewarded crafters when others used goods was cut for database load (but see Crafting: a 2003 guide says crafters did earn XP when products were used; so a form shipped). Power came from gaining new capabilities (horizontal progression), not larger bars. — [Koster part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/); [Kebernet guide](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_039.html)
- Apprenticeship: players can teach each other skills by grouping; some skills require Apprenticeship XP; learning from a player needs no NPC trainer and charges no credits automatically. — [Manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)
- Starting: pick one Starting Profession at creation; other Novice titles are bought from trainers for a credit fee. You need not learn all four disciplines; all four are needed for the Master title. Topping a discipline may open an Elite Profession or a title. — [Manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)
- Surrender/respec: select a skill in the Skills screen and choose Surrender; you cannot surrender a skill that is a prerequisite of one you still hold (so only the top of a line); you do NOT regain XP or credits spent; you must re-earn XP and pay again. Wiki says points are returned (capacity yes, XP no). — [Manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html); [Skill points wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Skill_points)
- Elite profession unlock = a specific title/box, enough of the right XP, and a visit to the trainer. Examples from the manual: Rifleman needs Rifle Specialist (Marksman); Pistoleer needs Pistol Specialist; Carbineer needs Carbine Specialist; Swordsman needs Master Two-Handed Sword Specialization (Brawler); Fencer needs Master One-Hand Sword; Pikeman needs Master Polearm; Tera Kasi Artist needs Master Unarmed; Ranger needs Master Scout; Doctor needs Master Medic; Dancer needs Master Dancing; Musician needs Master Musicianship; Image Designer needs Hairstyling Specialist; Creature Handler needs two Scout boxes (Exploration IV and Hunting IV). All Artisan crafter elites hang off Artisan boxes: Architect, Armorsmith, Droid Engineer, Weaponsmith all need Engineering IV: Complex Systems; Chef and Tailor need Domestic Arts IV: Clothing Repair. — [Manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)
- Hybrids ("broader base", often need masters in two or more professions): Bounty Hunter = Master Marksman + Master Scout; Combat Medic = Ranged Support Specialist (Marksman) + Master Medic; Commando = Master Marksman + Unarmed Specialist (Brawler) + Engineering Specialist (Artisan); Smuggler = Pistol Specialist + Business IV + Unarmed Specialist + Engineering Specialist; Squad Leader = Ranged Weapons Support (Marksman) + Survival IV (Scout); Bio-Engineer = Organic Chemistry IV (Medic) + Hunting IV (Scout). — [Manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)
- Politician is a zero-skill-point profession (players keep their other templates); on pre-CU servers it gained XP weekly with city updates and per election vote. Same pattern for Jump to Lightspeed pilots. — [SWG Wiki: Politician (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Politician)
- Koster says hybrid builds were intentional; the later NGE collapsed this to 9 professions (Jedi, Bounty Hunter, Smuggler, Commando, Spy, Officer, Medic, Entertainer, Trader) with Pilot, Chronicler, Politician as side professions. — [Koster part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)
- Koster on later trouble: balancing 30+ skill trees was a constant struggle; the class system was seen as unsustainable (reasons given for CU). — [MMO Folklorist](https://mmofolklorist.com/2023/01/05/star-wars-galaxies-a-brief-history/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Core transferable shape for Loka: typed XP per activity, spent on boxes in a tree, a hard capacity, free-but-lossy surrender, and prerequisites that cross trees (elite = "top of a discipline", hybrid = several trees). Hidden elites can be gated purely by box prerequisites (Jedi was the only secret one).
- Capacity-bounded respec (lose spent XP) makes experimentation costly but not final; Loka should choose consciously whether surrender refunds.

### Gaps
- Complete list of XP types and per-box XP costs not found (manual says "described under professions"). Launch (June 2003) skill-point cap versus later values not verified; the 250 figure comes from the wiki alone.
- Which Jedi-relevant data differ at launch vs. pre-CU is not separated in the wiki.

## The hidden Jedi unlock

### Takeaway
Koster's ideal (a separate permadeath "hardcore" Jedi slot, hunted by bounty hunters, Boba Fett, Vader) was rejected with "Permadeath?!?". A security-through-obscurity version shipped: a hidden random list (first a task list, then a set of profession masteries). Holocrons (hints, added at LucasArts marketing's request) let players brute-force it; Jedi became a grind, which Koster calls the point after which "the game was dead". Publish 9 (2004) replaced it with a visible Force Sensitive quest/village path.

### Cited Findings
- The dilemma: everyone wants to be a Jedi; Jedi are rare in the film era; Jedi are powerful. Koster calls Jedi "an alpha class"; "My first thought was, 'make them NPC only.'" Weaker Jedi or another era were rejected. — [Koster, A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/)
- Original proposal: a locked extra character slot, "hardcore" like Diablo, starts weak, powers grow by use of the Force; use near Imperials/players can be reported, bringing bounty hunters, then Boba Fett and Mara Jade, then Vader; death permanent ("It would be rigged"); survival time on a leaderboard; a Jedi Master reward "Blue Glowy" emote summoning past Jedi ghosts on your main character. Response: "Permadeath?!?" — [A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-brilliant-star-wars-galaxies-idea-that-didnt-happen-1699219345)
- Shipped idea: Jedi as a "personality test": a secret randomized list of tasks across four Bartle types, with no notification on completion; Koster: "absolutely security through obscurity". Under schedule pressure the task list became a set of skills ("I said yes"); he calls it "a very fateful decision made at the end of a 14- or 16-hour crunch day". — [A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/); [PC Gamer via search summary](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/star-wars-galaxies-was-an-mmo-that-almost-changed-the-world) (quote relayed by search tool; verify)
- Mechanic as played: master four randomly chosen professions (the player is not told which or when done). Devs expected the first Jedi to take years. — [SiliconANGLE summary of Koster](https://siliconangle.com/2015/04/20/how-the-desire-for-jedi-killed-star-wars-galaxies/)
- Holocrons: LucasArts marketing wanted a Jedi "by Christmas" and hints; hint items told you one skill to master. "As soon as three people all have gotten a hint that what they need is to master a specific skill box, the secret was out." "Everyone started playing everything they didn't like." "After Holocrons, the game was dead; it was just that nobody knew it yet." Jedi became "a thing for grinders and achievement-mad powergamers." — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2015/04/20/how-the-desire-for-jedi-killed-star-wars-galaxies/); [A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/)
- First player Jedi unlocked Nov 7, 2003. — [MMO Folklorist](https://mmofolklorist.com/2023/01/05/star-wars-galaxies-a-brief-history/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)
- Fan "4+1 theory" and holocron being consumed on use, naming one profession: fan sources only; unofficial. — search tool summary of [SiliconANGLE-era fan threads](https://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070131231648/index.html)
- Commenters report many "hologrinders" quit after Publish 9 ("Secrets of the Force"); a temporary test let nearly everyone become Jedi on a test server from late May to July 2004; AFK macro grinding hurt markets in the holocron era. These are player comments, not Koster. — [A Jedi Saga comments](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/)
- Post-Publish 9 path (Jedi Path Revamp Guide v1.3, 2 Dec 2004, player-written): pre-P9 Jedi bypass it. Others master one profession (any, not necessarily elite), then earn badge combinations (1 profession, 5 content, 3 Jedi POI, 2 difficult POI, 5 easy POI) to be visited by "the Old Man" and become Force Sensitive; then enter the Village of Aurilia (Dathomir), which is attacked by Sith Shadows in four 3-week real-time phases (12-week cycle); each phase you take one quest from one of four villagers; FS skill lines (Combat Prowess, Enhanced Reflexes, Heightened Senses, Crafting Mastery) are bought with FS XP converted from normal XP at set ratios (e.g. Bounty Hunter 1:1, Combat 3:1, Weapon 30:1, Surveying 10:1, Structure Crafting 35:1); six level-4 FS boxes needed for the exit quest (defeat Mellichae) and Padawan Trials. — [Jedi Path Revamp Guide (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070127174504/index.html)
- Koster says the Village quest chain did not stop grinders. — [A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/) (summariser noted it did not tie the Village to "patch 9"; use the guide above for that)
- Permadeath: Wikipedia says permadeath was planned but removed before launch at SOE's request, and death penalty later changed to skill loss (Jan 2004). Conflicts in emphasis with Koster (idea was rejected as a design); mark DISPUTED/unverified. A 2008 SWGEmu FAQ says the Force Sensitive slot is perma-death on that emulator. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies); emulator FAQ per [search summary](https://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20080205174235)
- Wikipedia: Koster later called the profession-based approach a mistake since few players became generalists; claim not confirmed in Koster's post. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)
- Koster ends: "I never played a Jedi at all." Takes responsibility for "yeah, fine, skills is good enough." — [A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/)
- A Jedi Saga is also an essay in his book Postmortems, described as "an impossible design dilemma broke a world". — [Koster, Postmortems page](https://www.raphkoster.com/games/books/postmortems/)

### Inferences
- For Loka's hidden elites: the lesson is that hints leak to the community in an online Realm (three players with the same hint end the secret); a hidden unlock needs per-player randomization or a discoverable-in-play path, and must not require boring play.
- The offline single-player Loka can keep secrets cheaply (no wiki); the shared Realm needs a different gate.
- The bounty-hunter/permadeath loop is a good design for an optional hardcore slot, not the main route.

### Gaps
- No confirmed account of how bounty hunters actually interacted with live Jedi (post-launch "Jedi bounty" mission system) from a primary source.
- The exact unlock chronology (which publish removed the holocron path, test-server dates) rests on player comments and a player guide, not SOE patch notes.
- Haden Blackman and Smedley statements on the Jedi unlock were not found.

## Crafting

### Takeaway
Pre-CU crafting was a resource-quality economy: 474 resource types with attributes, survey and sample or place harvesters, craft from schematics requiring named or generic resources, experiment with a risk/reward point system, mass-produce through factories, and sell via player vendors; items decayed and needed repair, driving demand.

### Cited Findings
- Resources: 79 subclasses and 474 types, randomly rolled and placed by Perlin noise; mined-out resources disappear permanently ("obsolescence"); crafting uses resource stats and experimentation rolls; blueprints call for specific resources and factories mass-produce only while those resources exist; items decay and need repair (repair may need scarce materials); harvesters run asynchronously and must be emptied; crafters could know unlimited recipes so high crafters dominated component markets. — [Koster part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)
- Metal resources use an inheritance tree (Ferrous to Iron, Non-Ferrous to Copper), each with stats used by crafting; resources placed over the whole map so harvesters can go anywhere. Koster's reply: a half-life on resources could have cleared old ones (database bloat). — [Koster, SWG's Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/)
- Surveying (2003 guide): five survey tool types (Mineral, Gas, Water, Chemical, Flora); survey shows concentration % at points and creates a waypoint to the best; sample with a Sample command (failures, critical success +20-40%); narrow range to find the "sweet spot"; hides/bone/meat harvested by Scouts. — [Kebernet guide (2003-04)](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_039.html)
- Harvesters: mine when offline; actual rate = concentration x harvester max rate; you pay a maintenance pool by operational time (so place on high concentration), need power, take output from a hopper; must be 1024 m from city centers. — [Kebernet guide](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_039.html)
- Crafting complexity: schematic complexity gates tools (<=15 generic tool; <=20 specialised tool; <=25 specialised tool near a public station; higher needs a private station). Schematic percentages show which resource attribute feeds which item stat. Assemble, then choose prototype, manufacturing schematic, or experiment; more points per attempt means more failure risk; more tries raise final complexity. — [Kebernet guide](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_039.html)
- Experimentation process (beta forum post, 2003): 4-pane window with experiment points (per item, unspent points can be kept), risk-of-failure percent; spreading points over several attributes in one go "pretty much guarantees failure"; resource quality caps how high you can go. — [Experimentation 101 (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_035.html)
- Resource quality attributes in the original game included OQ (Overall Quality), CD, CR, DR, FL, HR, MA, PE, SR, UT; the list comes from SWG Restoration, a fan emulator (NOT original) — treat as indicative. Resources there spawn and deplete on random schedules and never respawn by name. — [SWGR resources](https://swgr.org/wiki/crafting_resources/) (EMULATOR)
- Factories: a manufacturing schematic made at a station, loaded into a factory with identical components; produces about one item per minute until input or schematic charges run out; output as "Factory Crates"; advice to over-experiment on the schematic since it is copied. — [Kebernet guide](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_039.html)
- Crafter XP: most XP at craft time; "UXP" when others use your products; durables give XP over time; consumables on consumption. — [Kebernet guide](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_039.html)
- Vendors: first vendor at Business III (Artisan); works like a Bazaar terminal; XP when people visit or buy; maintenance charged by item count; stores usually far away so shops need waypoints; vendor items count against Bazaar limits. — [Kebernet guide](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20071216103635/guides_039.html)
- Economy: all shops player-run; added skill tree for advertising and branding; commodities market deliberately not a full auction house (bulk resources and low drops); everything tradeable; NPC drops mostly junk; resulting wealth showed a Pareto distribution. — [Koster part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)
- Koster 2011: SWG had "Real economies complete with supply chains and wholesalers and shopkeepers." — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-06-24-raph-koster-on-the-legacy-of-star-wars-galaxies.html)
- Koster's lesson from live ops: an auction-house change was made by asking "how many Master Merchants are there?" instead of how many people run shops (wrong metric). — [Koster, Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/)

### Inferences
- Item decay + finite named resources + mass production only while resources exist = built-in sinks and perpetual turnover; the cost was database growth (Koster suggests half-life).
- For Loka (offline): resource attributes feeding schematic stat weights is a compact, text-friendly crafting model.

### Gaps
- Crafter name/serial on items ("crafter reputation"): NOT found in any source read. Kebernet notes naming your product, but that is not a signature mechanic. Needs a source (SWG wiki "Crafting serial / crafted by").
- Exact pre-CU resource attribute list and ranges (only emulator figures found); decay numbers; precise sink/credit-flow figures.

## The social web: entertainers, medics, interdependence

### Takeaway
Combat generated wounds and "battle fatigue" that only players could cure (doctors/medics and entertainers in cantinas/hospitals), buffs came from entertainers and chefs, gear from crafters, so that every profession needed others. Koster says the entertainer fatigue loop never worked as intended, buffs were overpowered, and combat itself was never prototyped.

### Cited Findings
- Entertainers (dancers/musicians) were meant to ease combatant stress; dance built from looped animations and flourishes; music used loops (licensing); a concert master could sync others. — [Koster, Living Society part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)
- Battle fatigue: PTSD-like; "After a while, taking wounds wore you down in a more permanent way" than medicine fixes; recovery needed an entertainer, tying combat to venues, musicians, a cantina barkeep, and a player chef. Intended cycle: buff, fight, return to camp, unwind in town. Koster admits it never worked since the game could not tell session length. — [Part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)
- Wounds could be healed only at hospitals and ranger-built camps (placed behind lines, so losing a camp cut support); field medicine could revive a stunned player. — [Part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)
- Doctors make medicines; Combat Medic hybrid; Doctor needs Master Medic. — [Manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)
- Buff professions were "the first stage in a social loop" but buffs reached about 400% (intended modest), undermining combat. — [Part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/); [Part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)
- Third places: bars, theatres, parks, wedding/guild venues. A PARC study concluded the game as a whole, not a single place, served that role. Mentoring was required for mastery of some skills. Cantinas and hospitals are placed in cities by Master Entertainers/Medics (see Cities). — [Part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/); [Player city wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Player_city)
- Koster: "players do, and respect, what the game rewards". Planned smuggling/bounty-hunting depended on player-created missions that never shipped: "a content-dependent profession is a terrible idea in an MMO." Writer/theatre professions cut (copyright). "We never paper gamed combat" ("a disaster"). — [Part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)
- Koster 2011: the social features that progression-minded players dismissed may be the legacy. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-06-24-raph-koster-on-the-legacy-of-star-wars-galaxies.html)

### Inferences
- Interdependence worked where each role produced a thing others consumed repeatedly (buffs, repairs, ammunition, food); professions with no steady demand or that needed bespoke content failed.

### Gaps
- Exact battle-fatigue numbers; entertainers' real-world uptake (reports of "AFK dancing"/inspiration buffs not verified from primary).

## Player cities, housing, and the world map

### Takeaway
Players founded cities by placing a City Hall; a Politician-mayor ran them; rank (by declared citizens) unlocked civic structures; Master Entertainers and Medics placed cantinas, theaters, hospitals; Architects built houses. Housing placement rules (circle terrain rules, exclusion rings around core cities) shaped the map, producing an NPC city, an empty ring, and an outer player "crust".

### Cited Findings
- Establish: place a City Hall deed on a planet below the city cap (20 on Corellia/Naboo/Tatooine; 50 on Dantooine/Lok/Rori/Talus); four more characters place houses within 150 m and declare residence in 24 h. Ranks: Outpost 5 citizens, Village 10, Township 15, City 30, Metropolis 40 (rank 5 and the cap table likely LATER additions; this page is a 2011 version). Rank unlocks gardens, bank, garage, cantina (rank 2), cloning facility and hospital (rank 3), shuttleport and theater (rank 4). — [SWG Wiki: Player city (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Player_city)
- Cantinas and theaters are placed by Master Dancer/Musician citizens; hospitals by Master Combat Medic/Doctor; cities update weekly; unpaid maintenance damages then destroys structures; City Hall loss disbands the city (about four weeks to decay). Elections every 3 weeks (Mayor can be challenged); city specializations (e.g. Research Center +15% experimentation, Manufacturing Center +10% assembly, Entertainment District, Medical Center) cost extra maintenance. — [Player city wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Player_city)
- Only Politicians can be mayor; they place cloning centers, shuttleports, mission terminals, trainers, decorations; Architect profession constructs houses (Engineering IV needed). — [Politician wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://swg.fandom.com/wiki/Politician); [Manual ch.5](https://web.archive.org/web/2007/http://www.swgemu.com/archive/scrapbookv51/data/20070128130746/manual_printview_035.html)
- Mayors chosen by ballot every 3 weeks; cities could add garages, shuttleports, cloning, hospitals, cantinas, gardens. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)
- Housing placement: players could place housing almost anywhere using circle-based terrain rules; suburbs barred around core cities (fiction reasons), leaving "an empty ring" of newbie monsters then an outer "crust" of player city; campsites gave town benefits in the wild; spawning depended on where players already were. Original city shops: no housing in the NPC cities made shops hard to find. — [Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/); [Part one](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/21/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-one/)
- Objects carry terrain rules (flatten, retexture), costing server computation; artists had a "rule budget". Procedural generation cut content cost; hand content never filled the frontier. — [Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/)
- Koster: pets, cities and housing were to tie players to the world; ownership keeps players. — [Part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)

### Inferences
- Rank thresholds plus weekly maintenance gave cities a built-in decay pressure and a reason to recruit; the Politician had no skill-point cost, keeping a civic role orthogonal to the build.

### Gaps
- Pre-CU rank structure specifically (3 vs 5 ranks, caps) unverified; city-specific lot limits, house sizes, and structural maintenance fees not captured.

## The CU and NGE: what changed, reactions, Koster's view

### Takeaway
CU (early 2005) swapped queued combat for a more active system and pruned professions; NGE (15 Nov 2005) replaced skill-box progression with levels and nine profession classes, twitch-ish combat, little notice. Stated reason: falling subscriptions and WoW; player reaction was large-scale quitting. Koster had been moved up to SOE-wide CCO and left SOE 24 Mar 2006; he says live-ops decisions on bad metrics, not one change, did most damage, and singles out an Aug 2004 group-XP change.

### Cited Findings
- CU: Early 2005; queue-based combat seen as sluggish; 30+ trees hard to balance; reception lukewarm, subscribers dipped, then recovered to pre-CU levels. — [MMO Folklorist](https://mmofolklorist.com/2023/01/05/star-wars-galaxies-a-brief-history/) (secondary)
- NGE launched Nov 15, 2005 with ~2 weeks' notice, after Trials of Obi-Wan (1 Nov 2005), which it made largely moot; SOE offered refunds; FPS-lite/twitch combat; skill points removed; nine professions; XP grouped into combat/crafting/entertaining; 45 Expertise Points (later CU/NGE era). — [Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/swg-nge-crying-freeman/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies)
- Smedley (deleted forum post): "With the game the way it was we knew we would never be able to attract enough people to really keep SWG viable"; existing players would have churned out. About 200K customers remained pre-update per the blog author. — [Edery.org](https://edery.org/?p=67)
- Smedley separately said CU/NGE were mutually agreed with LucasArts; and in July 2012 admitted "stupid decisions". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars_Galaxies) (the first claim via CBS interview per a search summary; verify)
- Fast Action Combat was designed by Jeff Freeman, a Koster recruit; forum claims of blame are player accusations. — [Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/swg-nge-crying-freeman/)
- Subscriber numbers: no firm figures. Estimates: ~300K at peak; MMOGChart estimate 170K in May 2006 (search-tool summary of fan/press compilations; weak). Koster: SWG reached about 1M units in two years, and high monthly retention. — [Koster, Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/); [Massively via search summary](https://massivelyop.com/2015/08/22/the-game-archaeologist-star-wars-galaxies-nge/)
- Koster on cause: "the bulk of the problems over time resulted from Live operations"; the Aug 2004 group XP change "doubled the churn rate" and churn stayed "50% higher ... ever after"; CU, NGE and Holocron drops came from good intentions to make the game more fun but with unintended effects. — [Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/)
- Koster says exit surveys' top reason was "Lack of content", linked to combat balance and lack of quests. — [Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/)
- Koster's position: he was promoted to Chief Creative Officer, so the team lost institutional knowledge and a clear vision; "I didn't really train a replacement". Wikipedia: promoted in 2003 and moved to San Diego, credited as CCO on 2004 and 2005 expansions. — [Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/); [Wikipedia: Koster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raph_Koster)
- Departure: SOE confirmed Koster left on March 24, 2006 ("interests ... don't match SOE's strategic goals"). He reportedly said he was not responsible for the NGE and is reluctant to discuss it (2008 Escapist interview, paraphrased by the interviewer). — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/raph-koster-leaves-sony-online-entertainment); [Koster blog, Escapist interview](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=17852)
- CORRECTION to the brief: Koster did not leave before the NGE. He had left the SWG team (promoted to SOE CCO) before it and left SOE four months after it. — see above.

### Inferences
- Lesson that survives: a skill-system rewrite was driven by growth pressure and retention data, but Koster blames bad metrics and a loss of design stewardship; skill-tree flexibility was real value that the NGE traded away for accessibility.

## Lessons Koster explicitly gives

### Cited Findings
- Choose metrics carefully; convey vision clearly; don't change without reliable data ("flying in the fog with bad instruments"); a license does not guarantee success. — [Did SWG fail?](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/27/did-star-wars-galaxies-fail/)
- "A content-dependent profession is a terrible idea in an MMO." — [Part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)
- Prototype combat early ("We never paper gamed combat"). — [Part two](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/22/designing-a-living-society-in-swg-part-two/)
- Security through obscurity does not survive a community; schedule pressure turned a designed experience into a cheaper one. — [A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/)
- Rarity and power cannot both be maximised for a thing everyone wants. — [A Jedi Saga](http://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/16/a-jedi-saga/); [AV Club](https://avclub.com/read-this-how-the-jedi-killed-star-wars-galaxies-1798278852)
- Procedural generation made the world feel alive: "we shouldn't necessarily know what was in our world". — [Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/)
- Hand-built content could not keep pace with player consumption. — [Dynamic World](https://www.raphkoster.com/2015/04/20/swgs-dynamic-world/)
- "I'd rather work on something with great potential than on fulfilling a promise of mediocrity." — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-06-24-raph-koster-on-the-legacy-of-star-wars-galaxies.html)
