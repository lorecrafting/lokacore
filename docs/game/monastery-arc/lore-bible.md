## The Monastery

### Name and Location

The **Tsering Monastery** (Temple of Eternal Life) sits high in the **Kangri Valley**, a remote mountain region accessible only by treacherous cliff paths. The monastery was founded over 500 years ago by the sage **Padmasambhava the Elder** (not the historical figure, but named in his honor).

**Surrounding Areas:**
- **Kangri Village**: A small farming community at the base of the mountain that depends on the monastery for spiritual guidance and trades food/supplies in return
- **The Haunted Caves**: Ancient tunnels beneath the monastery, sealed generations ago when dark forces grew too strong within
- **Pilgrims' Path**: The winding trail that leads from the lowlands up to the monastery, taking three days to climb

### Founding History

**Year of Founding**: Approximately 500 years before present events

**The Founding Story**: Padmasambhava the Elder was a wandering sage who sought a place of power to establish a center of learning. He followed a vision of a blue lotus in the mountains and discovered the Kangri Valley. The valley's natural chi/prana made it ideal for meditation. He established the first temple on the site of an ancient sacred spring.

## The Three Poisons

Central to Loka's Buddhist-inspired philosophy are the **Three Poisons** (*kleshas*):

### Raga (Attachment/Desire)
- **Sanskrit**: Rāga
- **Manifestation**: Raga Mara - demon of clinging desire
- **Chamber**: The Attachment Chamber
- **Antidote**: Non-attachment, generosity
- **Symbolism**: Gold, hungry ghosts, phantom treasures

### Dvesha (Aversion/Hatred)
- **Sanskrit**: Dveṣa
- **Manifestation**: Dvesha Mara - demon of wrathful rejection
- **Chamber**: The Aversion Chamber
- **Antidote**: Compassion, loving-kindness
- **Symbolism**: Red, fire, fury guardians

### Moha (Ignorance/Delusion)
- **Sanskrit**: Moha
- **Manifestation**: Moha Mara - demon of confusion
- **Chamber**: The Ignorance Chamber
- **Antidote**: Wisdom, clear seeing
- **Symbolism**: Gray/mist, mirrors, wisps

### Mara the Deceiver

**Mara** is not an external demon but the personification of the shadow within all beings. In the caves, the collective spiritual energy has given form to this shadow. Defeating Mara is not about destruction but recognition - seeing through the illusion.

### Sacred Objects

- **Butter Lamps**: Lit as offerings, represent wisdom dispelling ignorance
- **Prayer Flags**: Carry mantras on the wind to bless all beings
- **Prayer Beads (Mala)**: Used for counting mantras, 108 beads
- **Incense**: Juniper and sandalwood for purification
- **Offering Rice**: Given to spirits, especially hungry ghosts
- **Thangkas**: Painted scrolls depicting enlightened beings

## The Caves (Three Trials)

The ancient caves beneath the monastery contain three trial chambers, each testing the pilgrim against one of the Three Poisons.


Where:
- A/A' = Attachment Chamber / Raga's Core
- B/B' = Aversion Chamber / Dvesha's Core
- C/C' = Ignorance Chamber / Moha's Core

### Trial Themes

**Attachment Trial**: Phantom treasures tempt the pilgrim. Ghosts offer what they desire most. Victory comes through letting go, not grasping.

**Aversion Trial**: Wrathful shades provoke anger. Fury guardians attack. Victory comes through compassion for their suffering, not matching their violence.

**Ignorance Trial**: Confusion wisps create mirror illusions. Reality becomes uncertain. Victory comes through clear seeing, recognizing what is real.

## Writing Guidelines

### Voice and Tone

- **Elevated but accessible**: Use poetic language without being inaccessible
- **Show, don't lecture**: Wisdom emerges through encounter, not instruction
- **Respect the traditions**: Draw from Buddhist/contemplative traditions with care
- **Ambiguity is okay**: Not everything needs explanation

### Naming Conventions

- **Tibetan-inspired names**: Tenzin, Pema, Dorje, Drolma, Tsering
- **Sanskrit terms**: For concepts like dharma, karma, mara
- **Descriptive titles**: "The Hermit", "The Wanderer"
- **Place names**: Should evoke atmosphere (Heart Cave, Threshold)

### What to Avoid

- **Modern anachronisms**: No modern technology or references
- **Excessive darkness**: Stakes without cruelty
- **Cultural appropriation concerns**: Respectful drawing from traditions
- **Definitive metaphysics**: Leave room for interpretation
- **Preachiness**: The world teaches through experience, not lectures
