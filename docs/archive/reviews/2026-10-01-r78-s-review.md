## Requirements derived from the spec before reading the diff

From 04 §5.4, §5.2, §5.3, §1; 03 §13; 21 §10 and Time; 06 §13; pre-release-proof.md; command.schema.json `run_job`:

1. A newly scheduled job is due strictly after now. During an advance it must be strictly after the advance's **target**, not just after the due time being visited.
2. An explicit advance snapshots the jobs due up to the target, inclusive (hour 19 moves Bram).
3. Jobs run in `(due_time, stable_job_id)` order. Row, insertion or reload order never decides it.
4. Before each snapshot entry runs, its lifecycle is re-read in the overlay. A cancelled, completed or rescheduled entry is skipped (ineligible), never resurrected.
5. Every job runs in the same proposal, and each job root gets its own writer group. Invariants are checked once at the whole-advance boundary, and the advance commits once.
6. Going over `due_jobs_per_advance`, `pending_jobs` or `created_jobs`, or any fault, discards the whole advance, time and jobs included.
7. `run_job` is authority-internal. A client can never construct it. Its CommandId is derived under a tag of its own from job id and occurrence, with a known answer.
8. Jobs are typed persisted rows and survive save and restore.
9. Ordinary actions cost no time. A wait or another action's time cost is an explicit advance and drains due jobs. Replaying a receipt does no due-job work (§5.2 step 2).
10. Lantern: the world starts at 06:00 and Bram is at the landing from 06:00 to 19:00, at the green otherwise.
11. Behaviour becomes an authority-internal command (21 §10). Recursive scheduling at the same time is forbidden (§5.3).

## Findings

**S1 should-fix: the composes-with statement omits two couplings.**
- Where: PR #83 body, and `kernel/ts/src/rules/schedule.ts:13-37`.
- First coupling: schedule@1's `run_job` imports behavior.ts. It treats every job as an NPC daily-schedule job.
- Second coupling: run_job moves Bram with a bare `entity.transfer` that emits no event, so movement@1's `entity_entered_room` never fires for him.
- Failure scenario: in slice R, a ReactionRule or quest on `entity_entered_room` at the green never sees Bram arrive. Principle 3 (reactions chain on events) silently fails for every scheduled NPC.
- Why it is a finding: the emergence owner decision requires the statement to list every such place.
- Fix: list both. Either add a `ponytail:` note that job-kind dispatch waits for a second job kind, or ask the PM whether run_job should emit the arrival event. Not building either one is acceptable here.

**N1 nit: the ponytail note on the re-read is wrong, and the re-read rejects instead of skipping.**
- Where: `kernel/ts/src/world.ts:199-201` and `:222`.
- The note says there is "no re-read". In fact `schedule.ts:20` re-reads `status` in the overlay.
- A stale entry makes `drain` return the rejection as the result of the whole wait. Under 04 §5.4 such an entry should be skipped as ineligible.
- Unreachable today (see requirement 4).
- Fix: correct the note so that slice R turns the rejection into a skip.

**N2 nit: writer groups can collide.**
- Where: `kernel/ts/src/world.ts:224` and `kernel/ts/src/quest.ts:73`.
- Both number writer groups from 1. A decision with both a time cost and a quest delivery would share group 1, which 04 §5.3 forbids.
- Unreachable today, because only `take` emits `item_acquired` and `take` has no duration.
- Fix: one line, offset the drain by the decision's largest writer group. This is related to the Q review's carry to D.

**N3 nit: an expected value is computed by the code under test.**
- Where: `mobile/authority/local-story/local_story.test.ts:523-528`.
- The expected stored bytes are built with the kernel's own `encode`. A literal canonical string is needed so that an encoding drift fails the test.

**N4 nit: the loaders accept an action whose command is `run_job`.**
- Where: `kernel/ts/src/cartridge.ts:160`.
- Probe: an action `hurry` with `command: "run_job"` loads, and the GameView would advertise it. Every invocation then resolves to `unsupported_capability`, because of the schema.
- The result is a dead action. An authority-internal command should not be an action's command, in either kernel.

**N5 nit: the job ordinals are not in the spec.**
- Where: `kernel/ts/src/fresh.ts:14-19`.
- Jobs take initial world ordinals after the NPCs and items, but numeric-profile.md "Initial world ids" does not record this, nor the NPC and item order it follows.
- A second host has to reproduce these ids. This follows the precedent set for items and NPCs.

## Developer's open note: a wait across two of Bram's changes faults

This is compliant, not a Lantern defect.
