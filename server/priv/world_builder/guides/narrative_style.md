# Narrative Writing Style

Guidelines for writing evocative, immersive game content.

## Core Principles

### Show, Don't Tell

Describe behavior and environment, not emotional states.

❌ "She felt sad and lonely"
✅ "She stared at the empty chair across the table, her soup growing cold"

❌ "The room was scary"
✅ "Shadows pooled in corners the candlelight couldn't reach. Something scraped against the floor above"

### Sensory Grounding

Include specific textures, sounds, smells in every description.

❌ "The forest was peaceful"
✅ "Moss cushioned each footstep. Somewhere above, a woodpecker's rhythm echoed through the canopy"

❌ "The tavern was busy"
✅ "Pipe smoke layered the air. Laughter erupted from a corner table, drowning out the bard's third attempt at the chorus"

### Subtext in Dialogue

Characters rarely say exactly what they mean.

❌ "I'm angry that you left"
✅ "I kept your dinner warm. For three hours."

❌ "I don't trust you"
✅ "You'll understand if I count the coins myself"

### Action Beats

Interrupt dialogue with physical actions, not said-bookisms.

❌ "I understand," she said sympathetically
✅ "I understand." She set down her cup and reached across the table

❌ "That's impossible!" he exclaimed in shock
✅ "That's impossible." His hand found the doorframe

## Formatting Rules

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| No hyphens for pauses | Use `...` or em dash `—` instead of `-` |
| No hyphenated compounds | "gut wrenching" not "gut-wrenching" |
| Em dashes for interruption | "I was going to—" she stopped |
| Ellipsis for trailing off | "I thought maybe..." |

## Room Description Pattern

```
[What you see - the physical space, key features]

[Atmosphere - sounds, smells, feeling, time-sensitive details]
```

**Example:**
```
A circular clearing where ancient oaks form a natural amphitheater.
Roots have woven themselves into benches along the perimeter. At the
center, a fire pit holds cold ashes.

Birdsong filters through the canopy. The air smells of damp earth
and something sweeter—perhaps the pale flowers climbing the
northernmost trunk.
```

## Dialogue Writing

### Natural Speech Patterns
- People interrupt each other
- Sentences trail off
- Questions go unanswered
- People change topics abruptly

### Character Voice
Each character should have distinct:
- Vocabulary level
- Sentence length tendency
- Verbal tics or patterns
- Topics they avoid

### Subtext Techniques
- Say the opposite of what you mean
- Answer a different question
- Deflect with humor
- Change the subject
- Physical action instead of words

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Problem | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Purple prose | Overwrought descriptions | Simplify, choose specific details |
| Info dumping | Too much exposition | Reveal through action and discovery |
| Talking heads | Dialogue without grounding | Add action beats, setting details |
| Said-bookisms | "she exclaimed angrily" | Use action beats instead |
| Adverb abuse | "he walked slowly" | "he shuffled" or describe movement |
| Perfect characters | No flaws or tension | Add contradictions, blind spots |

## Tone Calibration

### Hopeful
- Light penetrating darkness
- Small kindnesses
- Growth imagery
- Forward movement

### Melancholic
- What's lost or fading
- Empty spaces
- Memory and longing
- Stillness

### Mysterious
- Unanswered questions
- Partial glimpses
- Sounds without sources
- Things almost seen

### Tense
- Short sentences
- Incomplete thoughts
- Physical discomfort
- Time pressure
