# World Design Process

A nine-phase process for creating story-rich game worlds.

## Overview

World design follows phases that build on each other. Never skip ahead without completing earlier phases.

| Phase | Time | Goal |
|-------|------|------|
| 1. Core Concept | 30 min | Premise, hook, central mystery |
| 2. Philosophy Layer | 1 hour | Real-world concepts to encode |
| 3. In-World Translation | 1 hour | Map concepts to lore terminology |
| 4. Worldbuilding | 2 hours | Physical world and culture |
| 5. Characters | 2 hours | Cast with arcs mapped to themes |
| 6. Story Beats | 3-4 hours | Complete beat structure |
| 7. Woven Threads | 2 hours | Interconnected narrative web |
| 8. Conflict Map | 1 hour | Tension throughout the story |
| 9. Implementation Bridge | 1 hour | Translate to Loka content |

## Phase 1: Core Concept

**Questions to ask:**
1. What's the one-sentence premise?
2. What appears true at first? What's actually true?
3. What's the central mystery players uncover?
4. What are the 3 questions this world explores?

## Phase 2: Philosophy Layer

**Questions to ask:**
1. What philosophy or framework do you want to teach?
2. What are the 3-5 core concepts?
3. What should players FEEL by the end, without hearing terminology?
4. What's the progression of understanding?

## Phase 3: In-World Translation

Every concept needs an in-world name that feels organic to the setting.

**The Failed Examples Pattern:**
- Character who had A but not B → failed
- Character who had B but not A → failed
- Character who had both but chose wrong → different outcome
- Main character who has both and chooses correctly → success

This teaches without preaching.

## Phase 4: Worldbuilding

**Questions:**
1. What is this place physically? How old? How big?
2. Who lives here? What do they call themselves?
3. What do they believe? What don't they know?

## Phase 5: Characters

**Questions:**
1. Who is the companion character? What's their arc?
2. Who are the supporting cast? What do they hide?

**Character Arc Pattern:**
Map each character's arc to the philosophy progression.

## Phase 6: Story Beats

**The 108 Beat Pattern** (or meaningful number from source):
- Act 1 (Beats 1-27): Finding Ground - player arrives, learns the world
- Act 2 (Beats 28-54): Deepening - relationships form, mystery grows
- Act 3 (Beats 55-81): Revelation - truth emerges, stakes rise
- Act 4 (Beats 82-100): Crisis - the choice, the sacrifice
- Epilogue (Beats 101-108): Grief → Joy transformation

**For each beat:**
- What happens (action)
- What player learns (information)
- What player feels (emotion)
- What threads connect here (weaving)

## Phase 7: Woven Threads

**The Six Thread Types:**
1. **Hidden Stories**: Parallel narratives revealed gradually
2. **Physical Artifacts**: Objects that carry meaning
3. **Echoing Phrases**: Lines repeated with new meaning
4. **Visual/Sensory Echoes**: Details that recur meaningfully
5. **Mirror Moments**: Scenes that rhyme
6. **Character Revelations**: Backstories in stages

Every thread needs at least 3 touchpoints: plant, develop, payoff.

## Phase 8: Conflict Map

**Conflict Types:**
- Interpersonal (character vs. character)
- Internal (character vs. self)
- External (character vs. world)
- Philosophical (character vs. belief)

Track tension level (1-10) across beats. Ensure peaks and valleys.

## Phase 9: Implementation Bridge

Map design to Loka content:
- Zones → YAML zone files
- NPCs → Prototype files
- Quests → Quest YAML
- Dialogues → Dialogue trees
- Items → Item prototypes
- Scripts → Behavior scripts

## Anti-Patterns

| Don't | Do Instead |
|-------|------------|
| Lecture about philosophy | Encode in story and character |
| Use explicit terminology | Create in-world language |
| Have companion explain | Let player discover |
| Front-load exposition | Reveal gradually |
| Make "right" choice obvious | Present genuine dilemmas |
| Skip the grief | Let players mourn before joy |
